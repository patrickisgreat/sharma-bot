# Limited Supply Podcast Transcript

Miranda Atkins works as a performance marketing manager for 365 Holdings, a company that owns and operates several e-commerce brands with vertically integrated operations. She oversees all paid advertising channels including Meta, Google, AppLovin, and Pinterest. Her background spans photography and the creative side of media buying, which she believes is crucial when combining visual elements with advertising strategy. She started her own photography e-commerce brand in 2014 and has been focused on performance marketing for the past four years.

The current landscape of paid e-commerce has shifted significantly in recent years. Miranda joined after iOS 14, so she never experienced the golden era of Meta advertising. Over the past year, brands have increasingly realized they cannot be entirely dependent on Meta. If a brand sources all new customer acquisition through a single channel and that channel underperforms or experiences issues, the entire business suffers. This reality became clear to Miranda when Meta underperformed in Q4 despite the brand seeing nearly 50% month-over-month growth throughout the year. The brand struggled to find scale on Meta during that period, prompting Miranda to explore other advertising platforms like AppLovin and Pinterest.

The approach to channel diversification involves spreading investment across multiple channels rather than concentrating everything in one place. By reaching customers across different platforms where they spend time, brands create reinforcing effects. If a customer first encounters an ad on Pinterest, they can be retargeted on Meta, and vice versa. This creates a compounding effect where the entire system works together. The philosophy is that customers don't live in just one corner of the internet but rather in multiple locations.

When measuring success across these channels, especially those that are view-through heavy, Miranda focuses on click data and ROAS rather than platform-specific metrics. She doesn't rely heavily on view-through attribution, believing that everything must ultimately make sense from a click data perspective. She looks at longer lookback windows of 28 days, understanding that there's often lag time between the initial click and conversion. If a campaign shows a one ROAS on day one and compounds to a two ROAS by day 28, that's worth scaling.

Cuddle Clones operates with two main hero products: the Cuddle Clone itself, a handmade replica of a customer's stuffed animal priced around $200-$250, and pet-face pajamas, which are particularly popular during holiday season. When testing new platforms, the team typically tests both products. However, different platforms perform better with different offerings. Pinterest audiences might purchase expensive home goods but hesitate at a $200 gift, while AppLovin users were particularly responsive to pajamas. The higher ticket Cuddle Clone item sees more success on AppLovin despite impulse purchases being easier at that price point on mobile games, but customers often return later to purchase the main product after researching the brand.

One significant discovery involves creating platform-specific creative rather than simply repurposing assets across channels. What works on Meta doesn't automatically work elsewhere. AppLovin requires a fundamentally different approach because users know they're watching an advertisement and cannot skip it. This means the creative strategy changes entirely. With Meta, the approach often involves trying to stop users from scrolling and capturing their attention quickly. With AppLovin's 30-second unskippable ads, the strategy flips to being more direct, information-heavy, and direct-response focused. You have 30 full seconds to tell someone about the product, explain features and benefits, present the offer, and create urgency. The consumption behavior differs too. Users click more quickly on feeds versus reels or TikTok, and people interact with different ad formats at different speeds.

Regarding AI implementation in creative, Miranda has tested various AI tools with mixed results. She's found that AI works particularly well for graphics but advises caution about using 100% AI creative, as end users can sometimes detect artificial content and may lose trust in the brand. AI is best used in the briefing process, where feeding detailed information about customers to AI helps write better scripts and improves overall creative output. As AI improves, this process compounds. Tools like Moby have shown impressive results, with the ability to create realistic-looking animated videos from screenshots in about a minute of processing time.

When briefing creative, Miranda has found success implementing AI-generated opening scenes of realistic-looking people introducing a hook, followed by collected real UGC content with voiceover. This approach works well for brands without existing filmed POV or UGC-style content.

AppLovin entered Miranda's radar during November when the brand faced challenges hitting goals despite phenomenal growth throughout the year. With Meta performance struggling and others on Twitter sharing similar sentiments, the CEO suggested trying AppLovin, which offered a credit opportunity. Onboarding happened just a week before Black Friday, typically the worst time to launch a new channel. However, the AppLovin team provided white-glove service, even messaging on evenings and weekends to facilitate rapid onboarding. The team spent the initial $10,000 credit quickly and provided managed budget support, spending through the budget in four to five days rather than the two weeks Miranda would have typically taken.

The click data from Triple Whale showed the channel was profitable and outperforming Meta ROAS within three days. The brand scaled from zero to $80,000 daily spend in seven days, and ultimately spent a million dollars in 30 days on AppLovin, compared to $100,000 on Pinterest during the same period. The creatives used on AppLovin were specifically created for the platform, not simply repurposed Meta winners. Videos that never gained traction on Meta were crushing it on AppLovin.

For creative production on AppLovin, the team focused on capturing the user's attention within the 30-second format by considering the user's mindset. Some ads directly acknowledged the captive nature of the situation, essentially saying users should stop playing their game to listen. Others used text-based formats like graphics of post-its or letter boards recorded as video, followed by sales copy. The team also mashed together UGC from various creators with clips and pictures, calling out product features and benefits. End cards proved most effective when clearly stating why users should click, such as calling out what makes the product unique and any time-sensitive offers.

For creative assets, the team mixed existing content collected for Meta with new material. UGC creative performed best, and working with creators proved more efficient than building a full in-house team. The approach involved sourcing content from creators, mixing it with in-house content and self-shot material to make everything feel native to the platform. Creative could be refreshed less frequently on AppLovin than Meta, with some ads from Q4 continuing to perform profitably through the current period. Adding new creative provides more learning opportunities for the platform's algorithm.

Regarding customer quality from AppLovin, Miranda notes that Cuddle Clones doesn't have significant LTV since customers typically purchase one custom product and move on. This makes new customer acquisition the critical profitability metric. Being a bootstrapped business means ads must work or the business won't sustain. This differs significantly from venture-backed companies that can afford to test channels without immediate proof of profitability. For many D2C businesses and small business owners, channels must prove themselves quickly or budgets get reallocated.

The current team at 365 Holdings includes a VP of Marketing overseeing high-level decisions, a retention specialist, a brand manager handling the website, Miranda on the media buying and growth strategy side, a creative strategist reporting to her, an assistant, and two people managing organic social and content creator relationships. The content creator relationships flow through a flywheel involving the creative strategist, content creators, and the affiliate manager. Miranda acts as the data analyst, taking learnings from performance data and deciding which creative iterations to pursue or which channels to prioritize based on ROAS and revenue.

The seasonal nature of the business, with Q4 being make-or-break for the year's success, influences all strategy. Everything Cuddle Clones sells is giftable, making Q4 critical. This pressure to perform creates urgency around onboarding new channels and exploring new opportunities. Most media buyers wouldn't expect to scale from zero to $80,000 daily spend in seven days on a new channel, especially immediately before Black Friday.

Regarding outsourcing, the company has begun outsourcing more as revenue scales. At a certain point, an internal team cannot handle everything, particularly during heavy seasonal periods. For a brand under $30 million in revenue, much can be managed internally, but scaling toward $100-$150 million typically requires adding agency resources or building larger in-house teams. The company has found good results with UGC content, which is low cost. However, Miranda hasn't worked extensively with agencies and reserves strong opinions on that front.

The relationship between brand and performance teams at the company is integrated rather than siloed. At their size, performance must sometimes dictate what's working for the brand strategy. Historically, Cuddle Clones brand guidelines emphasized cheerful and happy without emotional content. However, when viral TikToks showed emotional customer reactions and unboxing experiences, and this performed exceptionally well in ads, the brand shifted toward more emotional content. Performance data showed what resonated with customers, including those unfamiliar with the brand, prompting the brand book to be updated. The approach requires balancing what's on brand with what actually reaches and converts customers.

Looking ahead to Q4 and beyond, the company is experimenting with several new channels and tactics. One brand is launching on TikTok shops after having a previously set up but underperforming shop. They're committing to that strategy despite mixed initial results. Miranda believes in testing channels sufficiently before declaring them winners or losers. Snapchat is currently showing good results, though not the volume scale of AppLovin. Pinterest works exceptionally well in Q4 but underperforms the rest of the year, so it's being treated as a seasonal channel. The overarching philosophy is that every brand should diversify away from Meta dependency. Companies overly reliant on a single channel face existential risk if that channel stops performing, gets banned, or experiences other ecosystem changes. Multiple acquisition flywheels involving paid advertising, organic channels, affiliates, content, and other sources create healthier, more resilient businesses.

For staying ahead of industry trends, Miranda primarily uses Twitter as an information source, valuing genuine conversations with brand owners over publications that lag by two months. She values perspectives from operating entrepreneurs and experts actively working with brands. YouTube creators like Dara Denny provide valuable expertise. She also notes the importance of evaluating sources critically, taking agency advice with skepticism due to their ulterior motives to acquire clients, while brand owners and operators share insights because solutions are actually working. She maintains similar skepticism toward courses and content from people with financial incentives to sell something on the backend.

Miranda is active on Twitter and LinkedIn and welcomes discussions about advertising. She appreciates the e-commerce community's collaborative nature, where competitors seem genuinely interested in each other's success rather than wanting competitors to fail. This distinguishes the industry from others where competitive dynamics are more antagonistic. The community ethos of wanting everyone to win creates an unusually supportive environment where brand owners meet up to discuss strategy and share knowledge freely.
