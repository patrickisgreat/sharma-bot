# Sustainable vs. Unsustainable Growth in DTC

Building a successful direct-to-consumer brand requires understanding the difference between sustainable and unsustainable growth strategies. When you have brand equity and paid media working together, you want brand equity to lead and paid media to follow. Never let paid media efforts overshadow what's happening on the brand equity side. Think of it like spilling water—that's your brand equity. Paid media is the mop. If there's no water there, the mop won't pick anything up.

Many brands, especially VC-funded ones focused on distribution and marketing over product, launch and immediately start running ads on Facebook, Instagram, or TikTok because CPMs are cheap. They acquire customers for trial, but not necessarily profitably or sustainably. The biggest problem with this approach is that you're acquiring customers who might not love your product, and not everyone will subscribe for as long as you projected. The LTV calculations rarely work out as expected. When you rely solely on paid media, turning off the ads means your revenue shuts off just as quickly.

The sustainable approach is different. It's where content becomes central. You build experiences and create content that resonates with your audience. Consider the example of Jolie, a showerhead brand positioned as a beauty tool. Rather than simply telling people their water is gross and asking them to buy, they created a water report tool where customers enter their zip code and receive a personalized report on what's actually in their water. This makes consumers realize the problem themselves, and naturally, they come to the conclusion that they need Jolie. It's slower than paid media, but it builds a rising tide of customers and advocates over time.

Think about quality versus scalability. When you drag an image in Google Docs from the corner, the more you expand it, the blurrier it gets. Unsustainable growth starts with a blurry picture that gets blurrier as you expand it. Sustainable growth starts with a very high-quality image that remains sharp even as it scales.

Several tactics support sustainable growth. Building community over time with quality, consistency and good intentions creates what's called the "head nod"—that sign of recognition where people feel they're part of the same tribe. When a USC graduate sees another USC sweatshirt, they say "fight on." When Jeep drivers see another Jeep driver, there's a nod of acknowledgment. This can't be bought with money; it has to happen organically over time.

Creating content that supports your audience is another key tactic. Levels did this exceptionally well by creating content their audience loved, with Levels always present as a discovery opportunity. Ritual does this on their website, and Chamberlain Coffee does it through Emma Chamberlain's YouTube channel and podcast.

Collaborations and partnerships with brands at the same level work well. This is the "Pit Bull strategy"—when Pit Bull blew up, it was because he collaborated with numerous artists, exposing himself to new audiences. When two brands with complementary audiences collaborate to create something new and market it to each other's bases, both benefit from the association.

Leveraging existing consumer audiences through places like Product Hunt, Bodybuilding.com, and Reddit provides value in communities where your potential customers already gather. Next-level referral programs that gamify the experience drive quality growth. Clubhouse's approach was brilliant: when you refer someone, your name appears on their profile permanently, which incentivizes quality referrals because your reputation is on the line.

Creating value through tokens and NFTs is another emerging tactic. The Nelk boys with Full Send and Liquid Death created tokens where buyers had financial stake in the brand's success. If you buy a token for $100 or $200 and the brand grows, your token appreciates. This creates a vested interest in the brand's success, turning customers into active brand ambassadors who might even help with merchandising in retail stores.

Viral stunts that turn heads capture attention creatively. Last Crumb's extremely expensive cookie boxes, Skims' iconic celebrity campaigns, and Feastables' Mr. Beast collaboration leverage creative thinking that money alone can't buy. Smarter financing through platforms like Wayflyer or the Jeeves credit card allows brands to leverage capital more efficiently while building their businesses.

Earned media finesse—generating significant press coverage, reviews, and organic media mentions—is something brands like House, Brightland, and Judy have mastered. Alternative distribution channels like wholesale and retail partnerships provide revenue that doesn't rely on whether Facebook ads are working. Kind Bars in offices, Hint Waters in offices, and Brightland in restaurants like Sweetgreen add substantial revenue without constant reliance on digital advertising.

Creating content for existing audiences by appearing on platforms like Netflix or Bravo TV, as Milk Bar and Loverboy Spritz have done, builds distribution and credibility. Building FOMO through limited drops and scarcity creates urgency. Nugs with their plant-based chicken nugget drops and Mad Happy with their limited collections create excitement that drives trial and repeat purchases.

The common thread through all sustainable distribution levers is content creation. When done right, content creation nearly eliminates the need to constantly run ads. It brings new customers into your funnel while reminding existing customers why they came to you in the first place. A TikTok with millions of views that stops performing with new audiences can be remarketed to lapsed subscribers to re-engage them.

Sustainable customer acquisition creates passionate customers. Brands built on content and community—like Parade, Mad Happy, Feastables, and Chamberlain Coffee—create advocates who evangelize for the brand. You get four key benefits: the head nod effect where customers relate to each other and form community, high-quality reviews and user-generated content that generates earned media, genuine advocacy and evangelism, and purchases with purpose where customers understand exactly why they're buying and what role the product plays in their lives.

The takeaway is threefold. First, get addicted to sustainable growth. Paid media growth is easy because the dashboard is built for you showing dollars in and results out, but it's quick and transactional. Sustainable growth takes longer but builds a stronger foundation. Second, build features and create content to reach new audiences rather than doing what everyone else is doing. Think outside the box and aim to amass passionate repeat customers who genuinely benefit from your products and become your best marketers through word of mouth.
