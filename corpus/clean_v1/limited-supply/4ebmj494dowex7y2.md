# Limited Supply Podcast Transcript

Instagram has fundamentally shifted its strategy toward promoting reels and video content, essentially attempting to become TikTok rather than leveraging its own strengths. The platform moved away from the algorithm it built to implement a "For You" page similar to TikTok's approach. While this raised immediate concerns about advertising, the ads remain intact. However, users have expressed significant frustration with the changes. Adam Mosseri, the head of Instagram, faced heavy backlash on Twitter when announcing these updates, with users consistently demanding the return of the original Instagram experience. The new interface feels like a constant start-and-stop experience rather than a smooth scrolling feed showing friends' content. Stories now feature back-to-back ads interspersed with user content, creating an increasingly fragmented experience. The fundamental problem is that Instagram is trying to compete with TikTok on entertainment value rather than focusing on its original competitive advantage: the social network effect of friends and genuine connections. TikTok doesn't rely on the network effect of friends at all—its algorithm serves entertaining content from any creator, which is a fundamentally different value proposition.

The rise of memes contributed significantly to Instagram's transformation. Initially, Instagram served as a platform to share what friends were doing through photos. Stories added another dimension by allowing less polished content. However, when memes arrived, they became viral content that people would share and enjoy regardless of whether they came from friends or random accounts. This revealed that Instagram users valued entertainment content from any source, not just friends. TikTok capitalized on this insight by building a platform purely around algorithmic entertainment recommendations. Now memes originate on TikTok, and TikTok has become the cultural center where being "in the know" requires regular usage.

Instagram's strategy of abandoning its moat is particularly concerning. The platform's original defensive advantage was that all your friends used it. By shifting toward pure entertainment discovery, Instagram is sacrificing this network effect without necessarily winning at the entertainment game against TikTok, which has years of algorithmic expertise and cultural momentum.

Regarding Facebook's earnings, the company reported a year-over-year revenue decline for the first time in its history, a significant milestone indicating market challenges. Daily active users increased slightly to two billion, which surprised markets positively. However, management warned that third quarter earnings would fall short of revenue expectations. Shopify, meanwhile, laid off ten percent of its workforce and acknowledged that its earlier pandemic projections were overly optimistic. CEO Toby Lutke previously claimed the pandemic had brought forward five years of e-commerce growth, then revised that to one year, and finally admitted the company has reverted to its pre-pandemic growth trajectory.

When brands face headwinds from changing ad markets and supply chain issues, there are several cash flow strategies beyond traditional direct-response marketing. One effective approach is creating a newsletter or publisher arm. A newsletter built around a founder's expertise can generate significant ad revenue through sponsorships. Five thousand engaged readers can easily command $2,500 in sponsorship deals, especially in B2B contexts where ad dollars are substantially larger than in B2C. Daily Tonic, a health newsletter from 247health.com founders, demonstrates this model with 100,000 readers and sponsorships from brands like Morning Brew.

Event sponsorships and tastings represent another revenue stream. A small spirits company generated $5,000 to $25,000 per event by providing tastings to corporate clients and other brands. These events became increasingly profitable as they scaled. The company also threw annual events in New York where they charged brands $500 to $1,000 per table to pour spirits while charging consumers $50 to $60 for entry, resulting in $10,000 to $15,000 per event in pure profit.

Insert card advertising in shipped packages serves as effective monetization. HelloFresh and other shipping-heavy companies receive payments from brands to include marketing materials in boxes. Some brands like Naked Wines built their entire customer acquisition strategy around these insert cards. Payment structures typically work on a CPM basis, paying significantly more than Facebook advertising costs.

Subscription box placements offer another avenue for liquidating excess inventory while generating revenue. Brands can negotiate with subscription services like Bespoke Post to include their products in boxes. Full-price products can command substantial payments, effectively preventing losses on excess inventory while gaining customer exposure.

Regarding Shopify's direction, the company appears to be focusing on the wrong priorities. Rather than continuously pursuing fulfillment infrastructure where it cannot compete with Amazon, Shopify should strengthen its core software platform. Native features like subscriptions, ratings and reviews, loyalty programs, and post-purchase upsells should be built directly into the platform rather than relying on third-party apps. The company charges substantially less than Klaviyo despite being equally important to merchants' operations. Shopify could increase pricing significantly and add substantial value through native features that integrate deeply with the platform.

Shopify should also focus on offensive strategies to help merchants acquire customers more effectively rather than just managing existing transactions. Features like meaningful personalization based on browsing behavior and third-party data remain underdeveloped. The Shop app, while promising as a marketplace concept, hasn't generated meaningful revenue for merchants. Shopify should leverage the massive amount of data it aggregates across millions of merchants to provide competitive benchmarking and trend reports, surfacing insights about button colors, conversion rates, email effectiveness, and regional growth patterns. This information would serve both as valuable content marketing and practical guidance for merchants.

Shopify launched audience targeting similar to Facebook, providing pathways to reach customers through direct mail and other channels while taking a cut of transactions. However, the platform needs to deepen its offensive capabilities significantly to help merchants solve their customer acquisition challenges. The company should focus on the $10 to $50 million revenue bracket of merchants rather than just small businesses, as this segment has substantially more resources and appetite for premium solutions.

Publicly traded e-commerce companies have largely underperformed investor expectations. Boxed currently trades at a market cap of $136 million. Grove Collaborative, which IPO'd less than two months ago at a $1.5 billion valuation, has plummeted to $700 million in market cap, losing $800 million in value. Thrive Market and other DTC brands have similarly struggled as public companies. Figs and Chewy represent exceptions that have performed well post-IPO, but most DTC companies have declined from their opening valuations.

Many of these companies went public following a business model of spending heavily on customer acquisition, particularly through Facebook advertising, with the expectation that future acquirers or public markets would optimize their operations and supply chains for profitability. This strategy has not materialized as planned. Grove represents a particularly instructive example as it essentially mastered Facebook ad arbitrage during a specific market window but failed to develop defensible advantages beyond customer acquisition. The assumption that someone else would optimize everything downstream proved incorrect.

Wink, a wine subscription service formerly known as Club W that achieved $70 million in revenue, now trades at a $20 million market cap. Honest Company similarly illustrates this pattern—despite raising $400 to $500 million over its business life, it now trades at a $300 million valuation. The market has clearly spoken about DTC company valuations and sustainability.

Bootstrap businesses have historically achieved better outcomes than venture-backed companies in e-commerce. Examples like Dollar Shave Club, Tuft & Needle, and Movement Watches demonstrate strong growth without massive capital raises and subsequent acquisition or IPO complications. The bootstrap approach naturally forces disciplined unit economics and profitability from inception rather than betting on future optimization.

Outside of traditional performance marketing, several creative marketing channels have proven effective. Airlines represent significantly underutilized advertising channels for direct-to-consumer brands. Away Travel placed mini suitcases filled with toiletries in United Airlines business class, creating memorable brand touchpoints that customers keep indefinitely. JetBlue Mint includes curated collections of DTC brands in first-class amenity kits. Delta has partnerships with brands like Muddy Bites (served on flights) and Unfem (canned wine), while Casper mattresses appear in American Airlines first class. These placements command premium prices from brands while providing valuable customer touchpoints.

Transportation security checkpoints offer unusual advertising opportunities. MeUndies sponsored advertisements at TSA security lines with the messaging, "Don't you wish you had better socks on right now?" The timing and context make these placements remarkably effective despite their unconventional nature.

Sports venues and bathroom advertising represent strategic placements for relevant products. HIMS pioneered advertising in bathroom stalls and urinal stands at baseball fields, creating perfect context for men's health products. The brand also advertised behind home plate and on planes, combining multiple unconventional channels effectively.

Snapchat experimented with physical vending machines selling Snapchat Spectacles, the brand's camera glasses. Beautiful, branded vending machines created significant lines on opening days, generating buzz and early adoption. The novelty and physical presence drove consumer interest beyond typical digital advertising.

Manscaped rented a barge in Miami, floating it up and down South Beach with a billboard declaring "Got Bush" and displaying the Manscaped lawnmower trimmer. The campaign demonstrated bold, unconventional thinking that generated significant word-of-mouth and brand recognition.

These unconventional marketing approaches typically don't follow traditional ROAS calculations but excel at generating brand awareness and word-of-mouth among business executives and influencers. They prove particularly valuable for businesses preparing for acquisition, as they signal innovation and creative thinking to potential acquirers. While the direct financial return may be difficult to measure, the brand-building effects are substantial.

Several apparel and consumer brands deserve mention for excellence in product quality and brand consistency. The Big Favorite specializes in recycled clothing with exceptional quality underwear and undershirts. Otherland Candle produces beautiful gift-quality candles at the $35 to $40 price point, offering an alternative to vice-oriented gifts like alcohol and sugar products.

Western Rise manufactures exceptionally comfortable black t-shirts and socks. Jambys has created a distinctive brand identity around comfortable loungewear for home wear, with the clever messaging "Just Don't Do It" as a counterpoint to traditional activewear marketing.

Kuyaya represents a particularly instructive brand study in consistency. The women's apparel company maintains remarkable consistency in photography style across photo shoots separated by years. Their images from yesterday appear visually identical to shoots from three or five years ago, demonstrating strong art direction and brand discipline. This consistency represents a key performance indicator for established brands—the ability to maintain visual coherence over extended timeframes signals strong brand management.

A CMO from a major corporation noted that American Crew, a hair gel brand found in salons, maintains this same visual consistency across decades of photography. The insight is that mature, well-managed brands commit to consistent visual language as a primary brand KPI rather than obsessing over daily ROAS metrics. This longer-term thinking about brand consistency and recognition differentiates established brands from growth-stage companies.

Kuyaya's brand motto "Fewer, Better Things" encapsulates this philosophy effectively. The brand has maintained physical retail locations including a beautiful store in San Francisco near Prince Street Pizza.
