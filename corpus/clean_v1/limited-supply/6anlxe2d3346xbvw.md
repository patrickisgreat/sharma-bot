## Incrementality, Attribution, and Media Mix Modeling

Cody breaks down three different approaches to understanding marketing performance. Attribution, typically click-based, tries to give credit for conversions driven by specific ad channels or units. Media mix modeling (MMM) is advanced regression analysis attempting to understand correlations between spending on different channels and revenue. It's traditionally done quarterly by companies like Nielsen and works best for larger companies, particularly those with retail distribution where click-based attribution doesn't work. However, MMM is slow and expensive, and most providers don't serve digitally-native companies well.

Incrementality is the most scientific testing approach. It answers the fundamental question: which conversions would happen anyway versus which ones wouldn't occur without a specific ad? The coffee shop analogy illustrates this perfectly. If someone stands by the door with a sign, every customer passes it, but that doesn't mean the sign caused them to buy. If that person claims ten customers came in and they only spent a dollar, that's a 10x ROAS, but it's not something you can scale. Meanwhile, someone with a sign around the corner might only see three people, but all three might be people who would have never heard of the coffee shop otherwise.

To run an incrementality test, you create a controlled environment with a holdout or control group. Let's say you want to test YouTube ads. You'd run the same media across the entire country on Meta, TikTok, Google, email, and SMS to keep it consistent. The only variable change is YouTube, run in part of the country (the treatment or exposed group) while another part gets zero YouTube spending (the holdout or control group). If the holdout generates $100,000 and the treatment group generates $120,000 with $10,000 spent on YouTube, you have $20,000 of incremental revenue for a 2.0 incremental ROAS. This gives you high confidence that YouTube drove that value since everything else was held constant.

These holdouts are designed to be statistically equivalent rather than arbitrary geographic splits. Tools like House use data science to ensure the groups are comparable while randomizing assignment. The major advantage of incrementality testing is its scientific rigor and the fact that it works without cookies or attribution, looking at actual revenue. It also works for retail and Amazon channels. The downside is that you can only run a few tests simultaneously, and they take time to complete.

At Jones Road, incrementality testing revealed that Google brand search, despite showing an 8x in-platform ROAS, drives zero incremental revenue. People searching for the brand are going to purchase anyway, whether Jones Road runs those ads or not. Understanding this allows the company to redirect that budget to channels that actually drive incremental value.

## Building an In-House Creative Team

Cody was initially drawn to building creative in-house because agency pricing seemed unreasonable. When he calculated that spending $500,000 monthly at a 10% of spend agency fee meant paying $50,000 per month, he realized you could hire seven full-time video editors for that cost. This made the financial case for in-house clear.

Jones Road has built a creative team that spans different specialties and purposes. The core brand creative team handles organic social, YouTube, and photo content. They have a different culture and perspective from the ad-focused team, operating at different speeds with different priorities. Recognizing that creative became increasingly important post-iOS, Cody shifted resources away from agencies and toward creative investment instead.

The team currently includes one full-time creative strategist, several video editors, and designers who work on the brand side but collaborate closely with everyone else. Over time, they plan to build this out into an in-house agency model with a dedicated studio space. They're even exploring ways to incentivize people, such as profit-sharing tied to creative performance, while maintaining caps to keep economics healthy.

The critical insight Cody emphasizes is that most of the industry focuses on less important creative variables like format, concept, iteration, and hooks. Instead, research should be the foundation. This means deeply understanding existing customers—what they care about, what they're saying in comments—but also researching new audiences you want to reach, who likely care about different things. You can't just look at your own ad comments since you're not reaching new people yet. Instead, you should spend hours on Reddit, YouTube, and other platforms understanding what those potential customers are actually discussing and using.

Once you have that research foundation, you can build avatars, create data around them, and speak to them differently. This informed approach produces far better creative than simply pulling inspiration from competitor ads on tools like Four Pie.

## Holiday Strategy and Black Friday 2024

Jones Road has refined its Black Friday approach significantly. Last year, they ran a four-day Miracle Bombs Mini exclusive promotion and sold 375,000 units, generating a million dollars in revenue for the first time with sufficient inventory. This year, they're extending the length of the promotion.

Based on learnings from a successful eight-day Memorial Day promotion where they hit a million dollars in revenue on seven of the eight days, they're evolving their approach. Last year's kit model, where customers had to purchase specific bundles, generated some complaints. This year, customers will be able to customize which shades they select, choosing from options like four, six, or eight items. Interestingly, this actually reduces fulfillment costs since they don't need to pre-kit inventory, though predicting which shades sell becomes harder.

The extended timeframe of the promotion was effective during Memorial Day, and they're applying that lesson to Black Friday. The goal is a million units inventory this year compared to 375,000 last year.

The broader holiday strategy recognizes that Black Friday isn't a single day anymore but an entire season. Cody emphasizes that people buying holiday products aren't discovering the brand for the first time in November. They should already know about Jones Road by then. This means the real work starts in September. October is the peak for TV spend, October features an anniversary kit that kicks off the season, followed by holiday kits that run through the end of the year. All customer acquisition during this period is intended to capture maximum value during Black Friday and the extended holiday selling period.

The team is doing significant testing ahead of the season, including checkout shipping options, upsells with AfterSell, and extensive PDP changes. They don't want to be testing during the actual holiday rush. They're also testing direct mail, sending 80,000 catalogs showcasing their holiday collection, which Cody is bullish about.

Additionally, Jones Road is launching European shipping on September 5th, starting with expanded European availability beyond their current UK warehouse. They're using Global E for merchant of record services and a 3PL in the Netherlands for fulfillment. Notably, they're not translating the site initially. Instead, they're treating September through year-end as a test to see which European markets respond best. If France performs well, for example, they'll invest in French translations for the site, ads, and emails. This data-driven approach means they only invest in localization once they've validated market demand.

## AfterSell and Post-Purchase Strategy

Cody has been working with AfterSell for about a year and loves both the post-purchase upsells and network offers features. Post-purchase upsells are now table stakes along with PDP upsells, in-cart upsells, and checkout upsells. He particularly appreciates AfterSell's flat-fee model compared to competitors who charge percentage-of-spend fees that add up and feel excessive.

The network offers feature initially seemed risky to Cody given Jones Road's strong brand standards. However, they tested it properly and found no negative impact on repeat purchase rates. The brands included (Nike, Hulu, Netflix, etc.) are legitimate, and the offers don't use aggressive timing tactics. There's no countdown timer creating false urgency. Occasionally, older customers might accidentally click add to cart, which customer service handles, but this represents a tiny percentage compared to the incremental revenue generated. Both the post-purchase upsells and network offers are now important revenue components with no brand risk.

## Final Guidance for Q4

Cody's core advice for brands preparing for Q4 is to get your plan locked in. Start with forecasting and inventory planning, especially if you're running exclusives. Know exactly what you have. Think about how you're filling your funnel—how you're reaching new people and what your channel mix looks like. Consider your communication strategy and, most importantly, determine your offer. Are you discounting? Are you doing something exclusive?

Keep it simple and understand that Black Friday is no longer a four-day event. It's a season that requires multiple offers at different times throughout the quarter. Execute your plan as well as possible, then spend the following year analyzing what worked, what didn't, and how to improve. The companies that succeed in Q4 are the ones that treat it as a strategic season rather than a tactical event.
