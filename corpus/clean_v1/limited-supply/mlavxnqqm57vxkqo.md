# Limited Supply: Building Creative Teams at Scale

Sam Springfield is the Senior Director of Content at Bark, a parental controls company that develops hardware and software solutions for monitoring children's devices. Bark uses AI and machine learning to scan kids' devices across social media platforms, Google Docs, text, and emails, flagging parents to concerning content including suicidal ideation, cyberbullying, and predation.

Sam started her career in executive assistance before transitioning to marketing, initially supporting her CMO at SalesLoft where she developed content marketing expertise. At Bark, she oversees all social content, organic social media, website content, and video content across the entire organization.

## Team Structure and Organization

Bark operates with a lean internal team of four people: a head of copy, two designers, and a social media manager. Beyond this core team, Sam leverages external partners including a freelance writer, freelance designer, and videographer. These relationships have been established over two to three years, creating a deep understanding of brand voice and priorities. Rather than thinking of freelancers as external vendors, Sam treats them as extensions of the internal team.

The company has adopted this hybrid model out of necessity, working within a startup mentality at a roughly hundred-person company. This approach allows Bark to maintain flexibility while ensuring high-quality output. When competing priorities emerge across the organization, having external partners on standby enables the team to shift resources without overextending internal staff.

## Project Management and Workflow

Asana serves as the single source of truth for all project tracking, managing work from internal team members, freelancers, and SEO agencies. The team operates on a sprint-based weekly cadence with quarterly milestones mapped out for roadmap visibility. Anyone in the organization can view the content team's board and understand current initiatives and focus areas.

While Asana is the official platform, the team has adapted to reality by allowing certain feedback to happen in Slack when timely responses are critical. Feedback from these conversations gets consolidated back into Asana comments to maintain documentation. This pragmatic approach balances ideal processes with practical speed requirements.

One key efficiency improvement Sam implemented was reducing the number of people involved in individual projects. By limiting feedback to essential stakeholders and one or two additional reviewers, the team accelerates decision-making and minimizes revision cycles. The lesson learned was that more voices typically lead to more opinions and more revisions, which slows progress.

## Content Output and Key Performance Indicators

The content team produces across multiple channels: Instagram, Twitter, LinkedIn, Facebook, TikTok, and YouTube. Beyond social, they create extensive blogs, tech guides, app reviews, and parental resources. The primary focus remains on social platforms and educational content.

Key performance indicators center on reach and engagement metrics, with particular attention to shares and saves. Parents frequently share and save Bark content, indicating resonance with the audience. The team uses Sprout Social for tracking and conducting quarter-over-quarter and year-over-year analysis to identify performance trends.

Video content has emerged as the highest-performing format. The team recently launched a podcast that generates raw material for bite-sized video clips. A single long-form episode can be repurposed into numerous short-form pieces, making content distribution more efficient while accommodating shorter modern attention spans.

## Balancing Brand Voice Across Channels

Bark operates in a sensitive space where messaging requires careful calibration. The company positions itself as a partner and support system for parents rather than a fear-mongering entity. The approach emphasizes education about real online threats while empowering parents to take action.

The company addresses two distinct audience segments: parents who recognize online safety as a pressing concern and the "not my kid syndrome" segment who believe their children won't encounter dangers. The messaging strategy attempts to meet both groups in the middle.

Platform strategy varies based on audience and channel characteristics. Instagram receives more restrained, educational content, while YouTube leans into bolder, more impactful messaging. This differentiation aligns with platform norms and user expectations.

Haley, the head of copy, has crafted Bark's voice since the company's early days. A comprehensive messaging and brand guide documents tone of voice, approved terminology, words to avoid, and approaches to discussing online safety. Most content receives review to maintain brand consistency.

To minimize freelancer revisions, Bark prioritizes hiring individuals who naturally align with the brand voice. The freelance writer, for instance, naturally adopts Bark's tone by reading existing blog content and understanding the company's approach. Her background as an editor ensures grammatical consistency, reducing revision cycles. The company was willing to re-engage a previous freelancer who had stopped working after recognizing the time savings she provided.

For designers, the approach has become more collaborative over time. After approximately six months, external designers grasp Bark's aesthetic preferences and design philosophy. The team increasingly welcomes designer input and original concepts, discovering that fresh perspectives often yield superior results to initial briefs.

## Influencer and Creator Partnerships

Creator collaborations maintain authenticity while ensuring factuality. Bark provides guidelines for paid creator content, establishing expectations around how Bark should be discussed. However, the company allows creators to maintain their authentic voice and style. Verification focuses on factual accuracy rather than tone control, as audiences trust creators for their genuine voice and personality.

## Press and Earned Media

Bark receives substantial press attention given its position in the child safety space. When collaborating on joint press releases or announcements, such as the recent partnership with Talkspace, the company navigates how to represent both brands cohesively while honoring their distinct voices and missions. The approach involves collaboration to identify alignment and willingness to request adjustments if content misrepresents the brand's positioning.

## AI Integration and Workflow

Sam initially approached AI tools with caution but has embraced their potential after learning to effectively prompt them. Claude is the primary AI tool internally, with Opus AI serving video content needs. Opus automatically generates clips from uploaded videos, creating thirty-second shorts or custom lengths with auto-generated captions.

AI supports content calendar development, helping identify gaps in website content by analyzing what parents actively search for. The tool generates SEO analysis that can be passed directly to freelancers as a foundation for blog content development. Rather than providing finished content, AI outputs serve as strategic inputs that accelerate workflow.

The key learning was realizing AI required coaching and context. Simply asking it questions without providing sufficient information about Bark's positioning, voice, and perspective yielded generic outputs. Feeding the tool specific website content, brand information, and detailed context enables it to generate more useful material aligned with company perspectives.

For design applications, Sam uses AI to articulate vague creative concepts. Rather than struggling to explain a visual idea to a designer, she describes it to Claude or ChatGPT, receives rough examples or inspiration, and hands those to the designer. This translation layer frequently produces clearer briefs than initial attempts.

When generating copy enhancements, the team uses AI to extract key takeaways from existing content, creating TL;DR recaps at the top of blog posts. This approach hooks readers with immediate value propositions while encouraging them to read full content. Performance testing determines whether these summaries improve engagement and page retention.

To maintain brand voice when using AI for copy, Sam links to specific existing content when making requests, coaching the tool to adopt established styles and tones. She links to pieces written in the desired brand voice, creating pattern recognition that helps the output align with company standards.

## Platform Expansion Strategy

The team recently began testing Reddit, recognizing that search results increasingly surface Reddit threads. Rather than delaying until significant resources became available, the team decided to "get scrappy and figure it out." Growth marketing and traditional marketing teams divide responsibility, with growth marketing leveraging various tools to ensure Bark appears in relevant spaces for SEO value.

The company identified specific subreddits and threads where Bark's parent audience congregates, sometimes in unexpected places. To facilitate authentic engagement, Bark is creating guidelines for internal team members to interact with relevant Reddit discussions while maintaining appropriate brand presence.

When Instagram and YouTube launch new formats like Reels and Shorts, Bark prioritizes rapid testing and adoption. The team discovered that Instagram Reels perform significantly better when created natively within the app rather than uploaded externally, despite being slightly more time-consuming. This observation prompted a shift in workflow to prioritize native creation.

## Overrated Practices and Underrated Tools

Sam challenges the tendency to pursue every trending content format regardless of brand fit. The instinct should involve ensuring trends align with brand identity and messaging. If creating trending content requires excessive conceptual stretching, it probably doesn't belong in the brand's portfolio.

Opus AI emerged as a significantly underrated tool in her experience. Few content creators seem aware of its capabilities despite its affordability at approximately two hundred dollars annually. The platform grades video content, identifies improvement areas, applies captions automatically, and offers various effects. It has substantially reduced video production friction for the team.

## AI and Employment

Sam rejects the premise that AI will eliminate content jobs. While acknowledging that some roles might be affected, she argues that AI functions best as a tool augmenting human creativity rather than replacing it. Maintaining brand voice, ensuring authenticity, and adding the final human touch that distinguishes content as unmistakably aligned with a company's mission requires human involvement. AI should be leveraged as an efficiency tool, not feared as a replacement technology.

Human creators add irreplaceable value through deep understanding of brand voice, strategic thinking about messaging, and the ability to recognize when content authentically represents company values. These capabilities remain essential even in an AI-augmented workflow.
