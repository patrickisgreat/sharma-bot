# Limited Supply: Black Friday 2024 Discussion

This year the discounts were meaningful and the American consumer is less scared, hesitant, and afraid than people had surmised. As a result, brands did really well across all categories—personal care, beauty, pets, and everywhere else. The sentiment going into Black Friday was skeptical given the economic news, but the actual results proved otherwise.

Shopify had an exceptional Black Friday, processing 4.1 billion dollars in sales, a 22% increase from last year, with an average order value of $110 and 15% of sales crossing borders. There was no reported downtime, no shop pay issues, and the platform performed reliably whether stores succeeded or failed was entirely dependent on the merchants themselves, not on the infrastructure.

Klaviyo and Stripe also emerged as major winners with no downtime reported. Klaviyo implemented 24/7 support through the Black Friday Cyber Monday period for the first time. Stripe displayed a dashboard counting all dollars sold through their platform from Black Friday through Cyber Monday, reaching 14.4 billion dollars by Monday afternoon. Extrapolating conservatively, if half of that volume occurred on Black Friday itself, Stripe processed around 7 billion dollars, making Shopify easily their largest customer, likely accounting for more than 50% of their transaction volume.

The scale of payment processing raises interesting questions about why Shopify continues relying on Stripe. With Shopify's market cap around 95 billion and Stripe's estimated at 20 to 40 billion, payment processing represents a meaningful percentage of Shopify's market value. Building their own payment infrastructure could theoretically add another 20% to their market capitalization.

Regarding smaller players and unique marketing approaches, most Black Friday campaigns were fairly standard. While some brands sent plain text emails and used strong subject lines like "best sale of the year," there wasn't much innovation. One notable exception was Bruma Ice, which used Tap Cart to power their mobile app and changed the app icon to advertise the Black Friday sale, making it visible right on the iPhone home screen. This thoughtful use of owned real estate effectively drove clicks.

Many brands underutilized their homepage real estate by only posting Black Friday discounts in top banners without prominent hero images. This lazy approach missed the opportunity to create excitement. Several apparel brands made significant inventory planning mistakes, going out of stock on medium and large sizes during the biggest sales day of the year. Brands like Nick Shackleford's drink brand handled this better by allowing pre-orders with promised delivery dates and discounts, still capturing sales instead of turning customers away. Western Rise faced similar issues, with Black t-shirts selling out and coming back in stock multiple times, creating frustration for customers who missed out.

The revenue performance varied significantly. One major brand processed 17 million dollars on Black Friday through Shopify alone. For a large brand managing Canadian business operations, the return on ad spend through Facebook reached 64.75x, meaning every dollar spent generated 64 dollars in revenue. This exceptional performance came from strong brand positioning and established consumer loyalty.

The brands with high average order values over $200 performed extremely well on Facebook, while lower AOV, more consumable, or supplement brands showed much lower efficiency, though still respectable results. One apparel brand achieved an 11x return on ad spend throughout the period.

In terms of advertising strategy, approximately 80% of ads from analyzed brands were Black Friday-focused or featured rewritten copy overlays on previously performing creative. Some brands took a different approach by not explicitly calling out Black Friday in creative, instead using social proof from the sale period and planning to run the ads through January without mentioning the specific holiday.

The email strategy proved critical. Brands that built large email lists throughout the year benefited tremendously, as notifications drove immediate traffic without requiring additional advertising spend. Several successful tactics included sending additional emails to people who hadn't purchased, adjusting sales to offer even bigger discounts on Saturday, and excluding people from email flows who had already bought. Implementing unsubscribe options that allowed people to pause emails just during Black Friday Cyber Monday, rather than unsubscribing entirely, would have preserved more subscriber relationships.

Facebook performed exceptionally well, but TikTok ads also showed impressive results in the second half of Friday and through Saturday, with row ads on TikTok becoming comparable to Facebook for consumable products, though not necessarily for high-AOV items. User-generated content in ad creative significantly outperformed traditional video and other formats, with the highest returns coming from UGC and static ads from well-known brands with clear offer statements.

Personal shopping experiences revealed interesting behaviors. Some consumers felt overwhelmed by the number of options and closed browsers rather than completing purchases. The anticipation didn't always translate to actual buying. Interestingly, one major brand, Hoka, didn't participate in Black Friday sales at all, which seemed like a missed opportunity given the strong consumer demand.

Tesla's approach of refusing to adjust prices for customers who had already ordered, forcing them to cancel and reorder to get the new price, demonstrated poor customer service and friction that could have been easily eliminated.

Nordstrom implemented an interesting feature in their app where looking at any item would generate multiple outfit recommendations featuring complementary pieces. This advanced apparel-specific recommendation engine far exceeded what most furniture retailers were doing, missing clear opportunities to suggest complementary items like chairs and rugs with coffee tables.

Looking forward, the key learning is that Black Friday consistently grows and shouldn't be underestimated due to economic concerns or skepticism. More intentional email list building throughout the year, particularly in slower months like August and September, provides the infrastructure for strong Black Friday performance. Rather than hoping email lists grow organically, brands should set specific collection targets similar to revenue goals, understanding that these subscribers represent investments that pay dividends during peak shopping periods.

The most effective approach combines strong brand positioning, meaningful discounts, reliable infrastructure, strategic email deployment, and thoughtful creative that emphasizes either UGC or clear offers. Brands that prepare inventory adequately, offer pre-order options when stock runs low, and maintain flexibility in adjusting discounts throughout the period captured the most value from the increasingly important holiday shopping season.
