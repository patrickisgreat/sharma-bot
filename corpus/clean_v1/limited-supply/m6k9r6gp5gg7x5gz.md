# Limited Supply Podcast Transcript

Jarrell is the head of growth at Tushy and describes himself as an obsessive growth marketer and operator who thrives on the challenge of growing businesses at various scales. He's grown companies from one million to over one hundred million in revenue, and emphasizes that the challenge never remains the same, which is what makes growth interesting.

When discussing the separation between direct-to-consumer and other e-retail platforms like Amazon and Walmart.com, Jarrell challenges the notion that brands should fight Amazon. Instead, he advocates for thinking about mental availability and physical availability, recognizing where customers prefer to shop. At Tushy, half of their business comes from Amazon and half from their dot-com, with minimal cannibalization between the two. Amazon shoppers tend to be Amazon shoppers, seeking convenience and the ability to stack purchases in one cart. Rather than fighting this reality, brands should meet customers where they already shop.

Jarrell emphasizes that at the mid-to-eight figure revenue stage, measuring incrementality across channels becomes non-negotiable. Tushy measures sales lift across various channels, finding that linear TV advertising shows higher incrementality factors for Amazon sales compared to connected TV or Meta. This makes sense because the demographics watching linear TV on channels like Fox News and ESPN align with customers who also shop on Amazon. The company tracks contribution margin daily across all sales channels using a combination of tools including Northbeam for data integration, MMM for monthly budget allocations, and MTA for intraday decisions, all feeding into a master Google Sheet that shows daily contribution margin trends.

Regarding Tushy's out-of-home campaign featuring trucks driving through New York City, Jarrell explains that there is significant measurement involved despite the seemingly unmeasurable nature of traditional out-of-home advertising. The trucks are geo-fenced with GPS tracking, and RFID technology pings devices within range. By matching these device IDs with IP addresses and subsequent website visits, they can create directional measurement of campaign impact. When paired with brand search volume tracking on Google and Amazon, they can see if search interest increases following the truck campaign. They also observe lift across all sales channels and social media engagement, creating a compounding effect that validates the investment.

The importance of creative production quality cannot be overstated in Tushy's marketing success. Jarrell credits their senior director of brand and senior design director with maintaining exceptional creative standards. He emphasizes the critical balance between brand and performance marketing. While growth teams want immediate results, brand guardians think about long-term memory structures. For Tushy, this meant moving away from problem-solution creative like washing fruit to represent cleaning benefits. Instead, they evolved toward more premium positioning that aligns with aspirations to deliver reliable, trusted products like Dyson or Apple. This balance between short-term optimization and long-term brand building is essential for sustainable growth.

The company looks at shareable metrics beyond traditional ROAS and CPA measurements. Post shares and shares per thousand impressions indicate what content resonates and drives organic amplification. One example of breakthrough creative during Black Friday involved a comedian partner who ran out of her house with a bidet seat around her head, hopping into a car with her husband for a date night. The absurdist humor communicated the product's importance while remaining brand-centric and memorable, ultimately outperforming traditional product demonstration content.

On the topic of innovation, Jarrell notes that Tushy faces the challenge of being a durables company where customer lifetime value currently mirrors first-order value. This isn't due to poor products but because they build extremely durable items. The solution isn't planned obsolescence but rather expanding the product portfolio with replenishable items like their unbleached bamboo toilet paper, room sprays, and toilet sprays. They're also developing new product innovations that extend the Tushy mission from gut to butt to bowl, creating subscription and repeat purchase opportunities while staying true to their core values.

For Black Friday strategy with a single SKU, Jarrell emphasizes returning to fundamentals. Brands must understand unit economics, identify relevant KPIs, create a compelling offer, and communicate it effectively. With one SKU, these calculations become straightforward. The contribution margin math involves understanding refund rates, discount costs, landed COGS, and marketing spend to establish target CAC and ROAS. This clarity keeps teams aligned without confusion about spending decisions.

Tushy doesn't run identical Black Friday offers across all sales channels. Amazon's cost structure differs significantly from their dot-com due to shipping expenses and fees. However, they ensure competitive offers exist across channels because the data shows minimal cross-channel browsing. Losing an incremental sale on Amazon due to lack of competitive pricing isn't acceptable.

The Black Friday advertising playbook involves keeping evergreen winners running at their current spend levels while creating new concepts. Jarrell recommends maintaining customer education messaging even during promotional periods rather than abandoning evergreen communication. Ads can be adapted with text overlays adding offer information, while simultaneously testing entirely new creative directions. Last year's top performer was an unexpected creator partnership showing the bidet seat being taken to a dinner date, emphasizing how important the product had become to users.

Jarrell's advice for Q4 focuses on mindset over tactics. Founders and operators should approach the quarter like the Super Bowl, with a clear game plan executed with discipline. A comprehensive calendar through Q5 2026, identified active channels, and understood incrementality across those channels should already be in place. The critical element is maintaining mental fortitude and avoiding panic-driven decisions that disrupt algorithms and create vicious cycles of constant changes.

He emphasizes not abandoning successful strategies simply because of nervous energy about hitting targets. The best operator for Black Friday is one who maintains balance, continues personal routines and habits, and doesn't sacrifice other aspects of life during the hectic season. This balance enables better decision-making and performance. Jarrell personally takes a trip to Miami the first week of December after Black Friday ends, maintaining that separation between intense work periods and personal recovery.
