# Limited Supply: Black Friday & Cyber Monday War Room

You know, I've been watching a lot of Star Trek recently and I love it when they're like, you know, the captain's always like, battle stations, and it gets my adrenaline rushing so hard. I'm like, let's go fight these Romulans. That's where I'm at right now with Black Friday, Cyber Monday. Yeah, so this is our emergency room, red alert, battle stations. It's Black Friday, Cyber Monday episode.

We're going to go over a bunch of stuff. First, we're going to go over performance data from what happened last Black Friday and Cyber Monday to help us understand what revenue looked like, what orders looked like, what AOV and ad spend looked like, and what emails from last year's Black Friday looked like. Then we're going to go over some creative marketing techniques and strategies for this year.

The data we're looking at comes from Triple Whale and examines nearly 10,000 stores, excluding any stores that do less than $250,000 in revenue per year, since that gives us a more relevant picture of actual businesses rather than side hustles.

In the 90-day period before Black Friday, these stores were doing about $7,000 in revenue per day on average. Over the Black Friday, Cyber Monday weekend—a four-day period—each day they did about $23,000. So that's 3.3x sales increase just during that weekend. Orders went from 75 per day in the 90-day period before Black Friday to 227 orders per day during the weekend. That's basically 3x increase in orders. AOV went from $93 to $101 during Black Friday, so a little less than 10% increase.

That AOV increase seemed low to me. I would have expected more from bundling strategies. You know, this is where you're like, save 40% on an order over $500. If you think about it, a 2x to 3x increase over your normal AOV should probably be your target for Black Friday. It depends on what the brand is. If you're True Classics, you can create a ton of different bundles. If you're Native Deodorant, it's hard to go through that much product that fast.

On the revenue and orders side, I was surprised to see 3x the entire weekend. I would have suspected that Black Friday itself does maybe 4x to 5x sales, with Saturday quieter, Sunday maybe a little quieter, and Cyber Monday around 2.5x. The hard thing is that to sustain 3x across four days is a lot. But these are stores averaging around $7,000 a day, so a 3x increase makes more sense when you think about it.

Let me talk about email data. We got this from Triple Whale and there were 11 campaigns per store for the weekend on average. That's interesting because it's hard to understand exactly what that means. You might send different campaigns to different segments—one for people who bought female clothing, one for people who bought male clothing. So that 11 campaigns number is a bit tricky to interpret.

The email metrics we did get were really solid. Average open rate was 49%, and average click-through rate was 1%. That CTR of 1% is actually pretty strong. Most e-commerce brands would be happy with that. The open rate of 49% is really good, especially when you consider that during Black Friday, all the normal segmentation rules go out the window. You're emailing literally everybody who's ever interacted with your brand. You're re-subscribing people who unsubscribed. It's Black Friday, baby.

When I think about it that way, a 49% open rate makes sense for an email where you shouldn't be excluding anybody. You should email literally everybody.

For ad spend, these businesses were spending $10,500 per day on Facebook ads the weekend before Black Friday, Cyber Monday. During Black Friday, Cyber Monday, they were spending $17,000. That's an increase of 70%. Google ads also saw a 70% increase. Pinterest saw a 32% increase. And interestingly, Pinterest was the only channel where ROAS got worse during Black Friday, Cyber Monday than before. That makes sense because there's just a ceiling to Pinterest that even Black Friday doesn't move.

That ad increase of 70% sounds about right because you're not just increasing budgets, you're also sweeping in all the dust that's been created over the last few months. You're buying the ability to get in front of more eyeballs who've interacted with you.

Now let's talk about strategy. The question is: should most of your ads change to talk about Black Friday, or should you keep your evergreen ads running and stack Black Friday ads on top? I think you stack them on. Keep running your evergreen ads that have been working, but add Black Friday ads on top. The evergreen ads that don't directly call out Black Friday but go to a Black Friday landing page can build incredible amounts of social proof through likes and comments. Some of those ads can become assets you run for years.

One thing there too is you want to make sure your existing customers understand the deal. The first job is getting the click. The next job is making sure that when they get to your site, they understand what the Black Friday deal is. Put it in a banner at the top of every page. They're on the cart page, they're on the product page, they're on the homepage, they're on the about us page. There should be a banner at the top saying save 30% on whatever this brand is.

I would also put a timer in those emails so when they open up the Black Friday email, they see something like 23 hours left or 20 hours left. That encourages people to click and make that purchase right away. You could also incentivize the first X amount of orders with a free gift. Write first 500 customers only, free gift, and everybody's going to feel like they need to purchase right now before it expires. You could even do multiple emails about this—first email in the morning says only 500 left, noon email says only 262 left, evening email you just remove the limitation entirely and write free gift.

It gets people to rush to you. There's only so much money in people's pocketbooks. You want them to purchase with you in the morning before their wallet's empty at another brand.

Now, if you're a small brand, here's a crazy idea: go into Klaviyo, get all your Gmail customers—maybe you've got 500—and send them calendar invites saying Black Friday email starts. You could use Gmail to filter out Gmail addresses and put them in the BCC line and send calendar invites. This probably won't make people upset yet because nobody's doing this, but if 20 brands did it, people would be pissed.

One thing to account for with calendar invites is time zones. You want to send them so people get the notification at a good time. If you set it for noon Eastern, East Coast people get the notification at noon, West Coast people get it at 9 AM. If you do it at 5 AM, West Coast people get it at 2 AM.

Here's another idea: if you're the CEO of a brand, send a personal note from you to your top 100 customers. Say something like, "You're one of our best customers over the last year at Sharma Brands. I'm the CEO of the business. I want you to know that I know who you are. I really appreciate you. We appreciate you. We're grateful for your business in 2023 and really hope we can earn your business in 2024." You could throw in a coupon, but even without it, your top 100 customers are going to feel incredible.

You could actually improve this by sending a survey to your best customers in late October, getting feedback on what they like and don't like, and then during Black Friday, sending an email from the CEO saying, "I noticed you weren't a fan of this, but we fixed it. It's now live on the site." This hits them right in the heart based on their own data and makes them feel heard.

Send an email to your top 1,000 customers with a VIP discount. Everyone's getting 30%, but VIP customers get 35%. They see the 30% email go to promotions, but this special note with the 35% makes them feel really good.

Consider working with direct mail companies like Postpilot. Send postcards landing two days before Black Friday with a code that works exclusively for them. You could even have robots handwrite cards saying something like, "Hey Moiz, this is Lauren from the customer happiness team. We've been going through our best customers and saw that you're one of the best. We wanted to give you this discount before Black Friday starts."

If you're a subscription-only business like Athletic Greens, this is tricky because you can't really run a sale greater than your subscriber offer. What you could do is target existing subscribers by offering them something in exchange for referrals. Say, "If you tweet your link and one person signs up, we'll cover your next month of subscription." It's a huge barter in your favor but there's something in it for them too.

Don't forget about churned customers. These people probably unsubscribed because they had too much product or just fell out of the habit. Send them a 50% discount to re-engage them. You could even say, "Subscribe for the next three months upfront and get 50% off." HelloFresh does this really well with their win-back emails.

If you're thinking about selling your business or fundraising, this is actually a good strategy anyway. Acquirers look at repeat purchase rates. CPR-ing back customers who haven't purchased in two years with a Black Friday discount legitimately boosts your repeat purchase rate for positioning purposes.

For email strategy during Black Friday itself, here's what I'd recommend at a minimum: send out a whole list email with your discount in the morning. Don't segment out the people who usually don't open emails—this is the weekend where that axiom gets thrown out the window. Email literally everybody except people who made a recent purchase and people with active subscriptions.

Then send a second email at noon to anyone who didn't open the morning email. Then send a third email at 6 PM to anyone who hasn't purchased yet today, regardless of whether they opened previous emails. Don't send fewer than three emails. If you want to send seven or eight, I can understand that too.

You can segment based on LTV or purchase frequency. Super engaged customers might need fewer emails, but people who don't buy that often should get more touchpoints through email since it's cheaper than text.

One thing we didn't cover but should mention is affiliates and editors. Make a list of all the affiliate editors and writers at Business Insider, BuzzFeed, and similar places. Let them know what your deal is because they're publishing hundreds of articles during the holiday period. Each listicle might have 50 slots. You might not get a high placement, but at least you get a backlink and mention in their listicle, which gets you exposure to millions of readers.

Activate influencers for Black Friday too. Everyone's going to be on Instagram looking at Thanksgiving photos and stories. If Liquid Death or your favorite influencers are posting about your BOGO sale, that's massive exposure.

The key thing to remember is: where do your customers go to find inspiration for what to buy? The Wirecutter, New York Times, specific influencers, Amazon bestsellers. That's where you need to be for Black Friday. Make sure your product is included in those conversations.
