# Limited Supply Podcast Transcript

Jeff Gundlach joined the podcast to discuss his career trajectory through various media platforms and his current role leading ads manager sales at Roku. He started his career at Westwood One selling national radio advertising, working with premium properties like Sunday Night Football and Monday Night Football. From there, he moved to the Big Ten Network where he learned about the value of passion and fandom in aligning brands with audiences. His experience at NBC Sports selling premium properties like the Olympics taught him about the evolving measurability of television advertising. He then made a significant pivot to Snapchat, where he learned about ad tech, measurement, and location-based advertising. This experience ultimately led him to Roku, where he has worked for the past seven years.

At Roku, Gundlach emphasized that the company combines "the beauty of television and the brains of digital," offering measurable outcomes similar to search and social platforms while leveraging the power of television as a medium. He explained how a DSP, or demand-side platform, allows brands to run omnichannel campaigns that start with a television story and then retarget audiences across other platforms. This approach enables brands to manage reach and frequency, exclude audiences that have already converted, and find new customers at scale while understanding the impact of their television investment.

The conversation shifted to how television advertising has become more accessible to direct-to-consumer brands. Traditionally, television required massive upfront investments in media buys and production costs, with results measured weeks or months later through MMM analysis. Roku's self-serve ads manager platform has democratized access to television advertising, allowing brands with minimal budgets to test creative, measure performance in real-time, and iterate quickly. This contrasts sharply with the old model where brands would produce a single thirty-second or sixty-second spot and run it for the duration of a campaign.

Gundlach discussed how creative has historically been a barrier to entry for television, but this is changing. Brands can now take their e-commerce photography and video shoots, add text overlays and music, and create television-ready ads without expensive production agencies. Some platforms are even using AI to transform social media creatives into television-ready formats automatically. This opens television advertising to brands that previously couldn't afford the premium production costs.

The discussion included several examples of successful brands on Roku. Lola V, a hair care D2C brand featuring Jennifer Aniston, saw a forty percent lift in overall sales during their campaign and a fifty-three percent increase in new customers year over year. Insurance companies have found particular success, as people may need insurance coverage at unpredictable life events, making always-on television advertising valuable. Food delivery partnerships showed that about half of new customers acquired through television viewing were either new or lapsed customers, demonstrating television's ability to drive incrementality.

Gundlach highlighted some impressive statistics about Roku's reach and scale. The company is the third largest operating system in the US behind only iOS and Android, and accounts for about half of all programmatic CTV ad calls. Half of all streaming households use Roku devices, and Roku's home screen is seen by one hundred twenty-five million people daily. The Roku Channel is the number one fast channel in the US and the second most watched app on the platform. These numbers underscore Roku's significant position in the streaming ecosystem.

A particularly interesting insight was how Roku's position as both the operating system and the ad delivery network creates unique advantages. The company can target frequent travelers by detecting when Roku devices connect to different wifi networks in different locations. This kind of first-party data collection, combined with third-party data partnerships, creates robust audience segments that enable precise targeting at scale.

The Shopify integration represents another significant innovation in making television more shoppable. Brands can now integrate their Shopify stores directly with Roku ads, allowing viewers to add items to cart or initiate checkout through their television remote. While most checkout completion still happens on mobile devices, this starting-the-journey approach is valuable for customer acquisition and legitimacy.

Gundlach explained his approach to pitching television to brands already scaling on social and search. Rather than competing with those channels, he positions television as complementary, providing incrementality through reach of new audiences and helping brands overcome platform saturation. The key value proposition is that television provides access to different audiences in different mindsets. While someone scrolling through their phone on Instagram might not convert immediately, the same person watching television experiences the brand in a completely different context that may be more conducive to consideration and purchase.

The conversation touched on how the competitive landscape for attention has intensified dramatically. With the proliferation of ads across all channels and formats, the standard for what constitutes an effective ad has risen significantly. Brands are working harder than ever to capture even a few seconds of consumer attention in an increasingly crowded marketplace. This makes platform choice and creative quality more important than ever.

Gundlach discussed how the role of VP of Advertising or CMO has shifted toward finding the growth person. These individuals bring playbooks and frameworks that can transcend industry verticals, moving away from the traditional CPG to CPG career progression. A growth lead who understands lifecycle marketing, ROAS, and LTV can bring that knowledge to any brand, running companies more like businesses focused on tangible outcomes like getting acquired or going public rather than chasing incremental market share gains.

The evolution from direct IO advertising with weekly check-ins to real-time, measurable, omnichannel campaigns represents a fundamental shift in how media is bought and sold. Television has moved from a brand awareness play with long measurement windows to a performance channel that can drive immediate, measurable results when the right platform and approach are used.
