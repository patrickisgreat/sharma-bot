# Limited Supply: Last-Minute Black Friday & Cyber Monday Checklist

Welcome back to another episode. Today we're diving into a quick, tactical, easy-to-digest checklist for Black Friday and Cyber Monday. I've compiled seventy points from all my previous newsletters on BFCM, organized into sections covering website tech, email, paid media, organic social, influencers, operations, and final touches. Since we're in the final week before the sale, this is designed to help you double-check everything before your sale goes live.

## Website and Tech

Start by testing every variant selector, add-to-cart button, slide-out cart, and checkout button. This is critical across both mobile and desktop. A lot of custom landing pages and collection pages behave differently on mobile than desktop, so pay special attention there. Make sure variant selectors pull the right color and imagery, that buttons add the correct quantities and selling plans, and that your cart displays the right cross-sell or upsell opportunities. Test that coupon code slots in the slide-out cart are functional.

Test all your promo codes thoroughly. For codes applied via links from ads, newsletters, or texts, make sure those links automatically apply the code without requiring manual entry. You can use ChatGPT to generate these URLs by providing your Shopify URL, the discount code, and your target page. That extra thirty seconds of work will incrementally boost your conversion rate.

Confirm that all discounts start and end at the correct time zones. The standard approach is to start sales at 12:01 a.m. Eastern Time and end them at 11:59 p.m. Pacific Time. This ensures that West Coast customers, who often shop late into the evening, get the full duration of the sale.

Update your homepage hero banner with your Black Friday offer. Make the message stupid simple—a visitor should instantly understand what the sale is even if they leave immediately. Also update your announcement bar at the very top with clickable messaging. Include two to three different messages and ensure they're formatted evenly so nothing looks odd.

Add countdown timers to landing pages and key product pages. While this might sound tacky during evergreen periods, it's completely appropriate when the entire world is focused on getting deals. Site speed is one of the biggest factors in conversion during BFCM. People don't have patience during Black Friday—they want to find their product, buy it, and move on. Test your site speed and optimize accordingly.

Verify that all your pixels are firing correctly. This includes pixels from Pinterest, Google, TikTok, Meta, Klaviyo, and any other platform you use. If you use tools like Blotout or Elevar, you can validate that your events are being properly tracked. For subdomain pages, make sure your data consolidation partner is aware of the traffic so they can route data appropriately.

Validate that your heat maps, scroll depth maps, and other consumer behavior tracking tools are live and working. Use Browser Stack to run cross-browser and device checks across iOS, Android, mobile, desktop, and tablet sizes. Browser Stack lets you test across all these combinations to ensure everything functions properly.

Freeze your code during this critical period. Unless something is mission-critical and will compromise your sale, don't make updates. Deal with everything else after BFCM. If you have duplicate SKUs with bundles, merge reviews across them. Brands like Caraway do an excellent job of this—all reviews appear across both individual items and bundles.

Validate the redirect logic for all your vanity URLs. These short, clean URLs should redirect to longer product pages with UTM parameters for tracking. For example, you might create apple.com/sharma that redirects to a specific product page with tracking parameters. These are much easier for creators, influencers, and affiliates to share and remember.

Ensure your back-in-stock alerts are active and configured. When products sell out during the sale, you want to immediately start collecting demand through back-in-stock notifications. Collect emails or phone numbers on the product page itself to get segmented, product-specific data. Consider offering a discount or guarantee (like promised shipping dates) to incentivize sign-ups.

Tag every new customer who comes in during BFCM with a label like "BFCM 2025" so you can track this cohort differently and measure their lifetime value separately. Finally, confirm that your Shopify scripts don't conflict with your discount logic. Test all your offers with all your active scripts to ensure you're not accidentally double-discounting or creating unintended consequences.

## Email and SMS

Prep your entire email schedule with the first email going out at 12:01 a.m. Build out segmented groups for people who didn't open, opened but didn't click, and other engagement levels. Set exclusions so buyers during this period stop receiving sale blasts. Add dynamic banners that show the current day's sale—when you update the banner once, it applies everywhere. Update your pop-ups to call out the discount prominently and make it clear that visitors are signing up to unlock the discount, not just for general updates.

From an SMS standpoint, keep your list more segmented since SMS is significantly more expensive. Plan for an announcement text, maybe a mid-sale push, and a last-call message. Pre-write three emergency emails: one for site issues, one for shipping delays, and one for products selling out with a waitlist CTA. These should be mostly pre-written so you can deploy them quickly if needed.

Add reminder emails about when the discount expires in X hours. Consider a founder plain-text email going out during the sale. Make sure to segment your VIP customers for early access and preview emails. Test all your emails across devices using Litmus to check how they appear in different email clients and in light and dark modes.

## Paid Media

Upload all your cyber week ads now. Approvals move slowly during this period since everyone is running holiday ads. Take your best evergreen ads, duplicate them, and add sale overlays. If you have evergreen ads that are performing well, don't turn them off—instead, redirect the URL within your website's CMS. This preserves the Facebook learning and prevents the ad from resetting. Don't change the URL in Facebook itself, as this will reset the ad's learning phase.

Scale your retargeting budgets proportionately with your overall budget increases. This is the time to hit people who have shown intent but haven't purchased yet. Make sure TikTok Shop is set up, TikTok ads are running, and your product feeds are uploaded and updated. If you're using a tool like Marpipe to run catalog creative and pricing, ensure it's synced across all platforms including Google, Facebook, and TikTok. Validate that all your Dynamic Product Ads are showing the correct sale prices. Check this both in your management tool and in ads manager to confirm everything is working properly.

## Organic Social

Update your bios with your BFCM offers. If you're running different offers throughout the sale week, keep your bio current. Create a pin story highlight called BFCM and add relevant stories to it. As you add new content, remove older stories from the beginning so people who visit later in the week aren't overwhelmed with older content.

Make sure your bio contains your sale offer and the link. If you have multiple links in your bio, it requires two clicks to reach your site instead of one. During BFCM, try to keep just one link. Use a vanity URL like apple.com/insta or apple.com/instabfcm to signal where traffic is coming from and build in tracking parameters.

Pin posts on Instagram, TikTok, and Twitter/X announcing the sale. Create daily stories that announce each sale, what products are included, whether there are bundles, how long the sale runs, and include social proof from other customers. Think of your stories as an advertorial or extended TV commercial—you have more of the viewer's attention here, so include everything: what's the sale, how long does it run, what's included, what do other people who bought it think?

Consider running an Instagram Live or TikTok Live during peak shopping hours. You can do this with simple phone setups or elaborate multi-camera productions depending on your comfort level and bandwidth. Record a founder video announcement on your phone announcing the sale and explaining why you're excited about it. Include personal anecdotes to make it feel like a friend talking rather than a company marketing. Put your phone down in front of you rather than on a table to ensure good audio quality. These videos have high click-through and conversion rates.

## Affiliates and Influencers

Email all your affiliates with their codes, auto-apply links, talking points, sale start and end times, and any other relevant information. These people often work from their phones doing everything from shooting to editing to posting, so make everything copy-paste easy. Format the email so even a five-year-old could find their link, understand when the sale starts and ends, and know what to say. Resend all talking points even if you sent them before—people lose things.

Consider rewarding your top partners with extra commission or other incentives. Some brands have given away cars or apartments as rewards. If you can get your top affiliates to go hard for you during BFCM, the extra incentive often pays for itself many times over. Make sure you're being completely transparent with affiliates about all the rules and details. They tend to move quickly, so if you miss the window to get them properly prepared, you might lose out entirely.

## Operations and Customer Experience

Prepare a comprehensive BFCM FAQ for internal use covering shipping times, carriers, return policy, and all discount information. Create a centralized document like a Google sheet listing every discount available, when it was run, and who it was for. This helps your CX team quickly validate customer questions about codes that might not be on your traditional BFCM list.

Set fulfillment expectations appropriately. On the marketing side, it's natural to want to over-promise and over-deliver, but make sure your CX team is under-promising and over-delivering. This prevents customer dissatisfaction when expectations aren't met. Flag expected order volume spikes to your 3PL well in advance using historical data from previous years.

Have your CS staff on-call and on Slack with a dedicated BFCM issues channel. Include representatives from marketing, web, and retention so you can quickly address any problems. Create an incentive structure for responding to customer complaints—for example, a $5 code for customer errors and $15 for company errors. Adjust these amounts based on your brand's approach.

Update your order confirmation emails with dynamic blocks to reflect BFCM language. Let customers know you're working as fast as you can and that orders may take longer than usual. Make it obvious that these are BFCM orders so customers understand there may be delays.

## Final Touches

Gamify your first five thousand or five hundred thousand orders depending on your size. Offer unlocking a free gift or bigger discount or a gift card for future use in January. Add a small mystery gift to orders to boost average order value. Market it as a mystery gift worth twenty-five or fifty dollars available at forty percent off—it's a gamble but a discount, which tends to increase purchases.

Have tailored bundles ready on your site and replenishment upsells or additional upsells on the thank-you page. If someone says no to the first upsell, have a second one ready. Consider offering both an upsell and a downsell option. Use the founder trick with both emails and Instagram ads from the founder's actual account.

Verify every single link in emails, ads, pop-ups, and text messages. Stress test your checkout. Every few hours throughout the sale, go incognito and do test purchases to ensure everything still works. Even if everything appears to function properly, something might break, so keep testing continuously.

Get some espresso ready, hydrate, text your team, block your calendar, and prepare yourself mentally. BFCM is the Super Bowl. Everything that goes right or wrong is your responsibility. Make sure you get it done.
