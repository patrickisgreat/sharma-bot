Jim asks about how to think about ad spend and marketing budget for testing new channels and tactics. As a rule of thumb, stick to test budgets around ten thousand dollars per test for media-related tests. If you're working with a creative partner, it might be different, but for media tests whether it's a new channel or a new tactic on an existing channel, the ten to fifteen to twenty thousand dollar range is good. Obviously, if you're spending a billion dollars a year in advertising, your test budget will be more like fifty to one hundred thousand dollars to show statistical significance.

Test budgets really depend on what your average order value is, how many purchases a platform needs to properly optimize itself, and how hard of a sell or how long your sales cycle is. Ensure you have the right ways to track the new channel's performance. Track directly attributed sales but also incremental sales driven in retail on marketplaces like Amazon, Etsy, or eBay, or in B2B channels as a result of the new media channels.

It is absolutely worth testing new channels, but do it systematically with a proper channel expansion plan. Don't just test whatever is thrown at you by sales reps. Google, TikTok, Twitter, and Snap reps are usually from the companies directly, but even then you get a lower quality rep when they're just reaching out to you unless you have real volume of spend showing quarter-over-quarter growth. Don't focus on testing whatever is thrown at you. Rahul asks about apparel advertising, noting that a lot of tactics don't apply to apparel brands like they do to other D2C brands. He's totally right. Apparel is focused on selling an entire catalog, not just a specific product, though companies like Everlane or Outdoor Voices have flagship products they focus on. You need to focus on what you're selling and what your focal point is. Are you selling the softest cashmere in the market? The lifestyle of wearing Xenia or Loro Piana? An innovative proprietary material that allows instant cooling while working out?

Apparel is all about building around that focal point, which is really about brand. Once you've got the brand out there, it's easier to focus on collections or individual products through more targeted advertising. Brand advertising should focus on the selling point or focal point of the brand.

Ensure all the other pieces are in order. Have a high converting website with the best UX and UI practices, and product pages with thorough information about materials, who the product is for, why they should wear it, where materials are sourced from, and why it's better than the same garment from another brand. If this information isn't on the product page, you need to be somebody like Louis Vuitton, Tom Ford, or Kate Spade with crazy brand awareness. Otherwise, when somebody Googles your product, there needs to be a ton of social proof doing the selling for you. If your product has six hundred five-star reviews, you can make a rule so products with over four hundred five-star reviews display that count on the actual catalog creative.
