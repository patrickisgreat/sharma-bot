Regarding measurement and testing strategy, we conducted an incrementality test with our client Lalo using House, which is incrementality testing software. Traditional measurement looks at bottom-of-funnel optimization toward purchases and subscriptions. However, when you reach diminishing returns where additional spending reduces efficiency, testing higher-funnel events like add to cart or initiate checkout becomes valuable.

Lalo sells through their direct-to-consumer site, retail stores, online marketplaces, and Amazon. We wanted to test whether running add-to-cart objective campaigns on Meta could generate similar or better efficiency than purchase objective campaigns. We set up a three-cell test: 40% traffic to purchase objective campaigns, 40% to add-to-cart objective campaigns, and 20% as a holdout.

While the initial Meta data showed purchase objective performing better, the three-week holdout analysis inside House revealed that purchase campaigns were only 2% more incremental than add-to-cart campaigns. This proved that for an omni-channel brand like Lalo where customers buy on Amazon and in stores, add-to-cart campaigns work extremely well. People add items to cart on Meta and complete purchases on other platforms.

We ran a similar test on TikTok, using 60% of spend on traffic campaigns (upper-funnel) and 40% as a holdout. The results showed just under a 12.5% lift in new customer revenue and good lift on returning customer revenue. This demonstrates that while purchase objective campaigns are default recommendations, testing alternative models through incrementality testing reveals hidden opportunities.
