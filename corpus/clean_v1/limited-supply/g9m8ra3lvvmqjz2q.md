Welcome back to another season of Limited Supply. I'm doing a lot of thinking and reflecting about how to make this show better. I want to try shorter episodes around the 30-minute mark since many listeners have requested that. I'm also exploring bringing back the fun banter element that people have missed, so I'm looking at co-host options. Most importantly, I want to make sure this is an enjoyable listen whether you're running, driving, or walking around the city.

Today, I'm going to cover something controversial. I'll be calling out tactics, channels, and strategies that I think are overrated or outdated. These are often from companies with great outbound sales machines, sponsoring events and conferences, or advice from platform reps who haven't actually run advertising at serious scale. The issue is that these reps are usually pushing what product marketing teams want pushed, not what drives actual performance.

Let me break this into overrated and underrated categories. I'll focus on underrated items in a separate episode if you find this helpful.

## Overrated Tactics

**Top of Funnel Facebook Ads**

If you're a brand doing up to $75 million in direct-to-consumer revenue, top of funnel Facebook ads are basically a waste of money. You don't need to run these campaigns.

The problem is that people think once they exhaust purchase-optimized Facebook ads, they must move upper funnel. But they only think within the specific platform. Growth marketing used to be all-encompassing ten years ago, whereas now people have become channel managers or single-function roles.

Top of funnel campaigns like reach and frequency, video views, website clicks, landing page views, and view content campaigns are largely ineffective. If you install a heat map tool and tag your UTMs with upper funnel campaigns, you'll see that traffic from website clicks campaigns bounces immediately. View campaigns show people viewing products and leaving. Add to cart campaigns show people adding products and leaving. These are not people coming back.

Facebook knows you're willing to pay for purchase ads and knows others are too. Why would they give you people likely to purchase when you're asking for site clicks or content views? It's not that simple. Top of funnel Facebook ads only work if you're a household brand name running site traffic or in-store shopping campaigns. You can't be doing $100-200 million annually and expect these to work well.

Instead, focus on upper funnel marketing that actually contributes: content creation, turning creators into evangelists, turning customers into evangelists, and creating content people want to engage with. Advertorials are the best form of upper funnel ads because you bring people in through an engaging story before transitioning to benefits and purchases.

**Programmatic Display Ads**

I started my career in programmatic advertising. I remember being taught that platforms like Criteo use 120-day attribution windows with view-through attribution, meaning people don't even need to click. The ad just needs to load on the same page. Within those 120 days, every other channel helps drive conversions, and then Criteo claims credit and reports a $13 CPA while Facebook shows $55.

Every programmatic banner ad company uses view-through attribution for their reporting because it gives them the most credit. They argue that people don't click banner ads; they view them and are reminded to purchase later.

If you're running programmatic display ads, you should either be spending over seven figures monthly on paid social, or running them to drive people to retail stores at massive scale like global retailers do. If you're doing less than $100 million online and running programmatic display to drive online customers, you're likely wasting money.

When I ran proper incrementality tests, I found that programmatic display on its own does nothing. Coupled with Facebook at high spend levels, you see some efficiencies, but not much. You'd be better off taking that $10,000-25,000 and investing in creators or generating new eyeballs in different ways.

Think about the last programmatic display banner ad you remember seeing. You can't, because our eyes aren't trained to notice them anymore. Most people have ad blockers too.

**Loyalty and Referral Programs**

This will upset some people, but if your business model is loyalty, it probably works well. I think of Best Buy, Sephora, Nordstrom with their credit card, and Amazon. The entire business model is built around loyalty. Best Buy doesn't care if you buy a specific TV; they want you to come to them when you buy TVs. Sephora doesn't care if you buy The Ordinary or Live Tinted; they want you to come to Sephora for serums and creams.

However, when you have a brand selling a one-off product like foot cream or a camera tripod, loyalty programs don't matter. How many times will you repurchase? Even with consumable products, have you ever been excited about your shampoo's loyalty program?

When I run the numbers, it's important to analyze the incremental profit from these programs. Compare that to the time your team invests, software costs, and development expenses. Most of the time, those don't add up.

The best referral programs aren't e-commerce ones. They're app-based like Dropbox and Uber because they're more native to user behavior. It's weird for me to be at dinner, talk about a Ridge wallet, log in, grab my link, and text a code to a friend. That's not natural behavior.

If someone built something feeling more natural, leveraging app clips or SMS, or using NLP with AI to understand text and automatically send contextual offers, that would be powerful. But most programs don't work this way, and people won't change their behavior because your shampoo company wants to run a loyalty program.

**Influencer Management Software**

I don't like paying for influencer management software. Many charge thousands monthly with 12-month contracts and no exit clauses, just to look up influencers.

Instead, I'd jump on TikTok, use the search bar for the people I'm looking for, and go down rabbit holes of similar content creators. Find aspiring creators and understand their network to discover other creators you might work with. Building relationships organically with talent you find this way pays off much better six to eighteen months down the road than blasting messages through platforms and hoping for responses.

**Honey and Capital One Shopping**

I've been saying this for years, and finally someone exposed them on YouTube. Honey operates as a two-sided business. To consumers, they promise a free Chrome extension that finds coupon codes. To brands, they apply to affiliate programs and try to set up custom deals.

Here's the scam: You give them an exclusive code like HONEY10 while your generic code is WELCOME10. Honey promises to promote you to their millions of shoppers, but they don't. They do almost nothing for promotion. If you track UTMs, their direct traffic is nearly zero.

What's interesting is that right at checkout, thirty seconds before someone purchases, they somehow attribute the order to a Honey affiliate link. Now Honey takes 10% commission even though they did nothing to drive the sale. If the customer uses the Honey code swap, they're getting WELCOME10 off again while you've already paid Facebook, Google, email, and text marketing for that customer.

Honey isn't using the code they're supposed to use. They found a legal glitch that should be banned. Capital One Shopping and most coupon extensions do the same thing. They don't even use the exclusive codes they're supposed to use, which also screws over influencers. This is why it blew up recently on YouTube.

My recommendation: Get coupon sites off your affiliate program entirely. This is 2024, not 2012. Nobody discovers brands on coupon sites. It's purely bottom-funnel. You should have your own deals page on your site or a publisher site full of deals. Some brands like Popery do this well, creating an active deals page that pops up when people search for brand coupons. That way, people see your best offers on your site, and you can clarify that other coupon codes are fake.

**Most Paid UGC Content**

Most user-generated content should really be called customer-generated content. The problem is most paid UGC from these creators looks like an ad from a mile away. If you scroll TikTok and see bad UGC ads, you'll notice comments are turned off because people are roasting them. TikTok doesn't reward shitty ads.

The better alternative is to look at highly engaging posts on TikTok, defined by comments, shares, and likes relative to views. See what highly engaging posts look like that are selling products through TikTok Shop. You can find top TikTok Shop brands online or switch your account to creator mode to see top-performing products and posts. That's what real UGC should look like, not the fake value prop congested content you pay $50 per video for.

When people run bad UGC and it doesn't work, they think ads or that style doesn't work. But the problem is partly that the creative looks fake, which people smell from miles away. It's also partly that there's no good offer, messaging, angle, or story. That comes from these creators just reading scripts without actually being active, consistent users of your product. They don't know what it feels like to use your product versus alternatives or how their lives have been positively impacted.

**Building Landing Pages Off Shopify with Scaling Pricing**

Building landing pages off Shopify with scaling pricing models is a waste of money. I've been building landing pages since 2014. I moved to Unbounce because the pricing was cheapest and it was all drag and drop. Unfortunately, I don't code, so I needed drag and drop.

Landing pages are UX-first, then UI. Speed matters. Good copywriting and content matter. But it's really about UX focus. In 2024, with free WiFi everywhere, why pay a landing page software with scaling pricing?

Everything for landing pages should be built into Shopify 2.0. At Charmer Brands, all our sites are built on the Dawn theme because Shopify updates it most and it's their latest. The benefit is that modules are duplicatable across pages. If a comparison chart works on one landing page, copy it to a product page in seconds. If a landing page performs well for Thanksgiving and Black Friday, duplicate it for New Year or Valentine's Day.

Shopify with good UX design and a good developer is essentially drag and drop. There's no reason not to build landing pages inside Shopify. Even complex custom application stuff can be built directly in Shopify. You can make headlines change dynamically based on UTM source or campaign name. You don't need to be off Shopify or pay insane amounts. You shouldn't pay more than a few thousand dollars to develop a landing page unless you're doing something very specific and special, and then you should own that page. That was the idea behind Hooks. You should pay once and own the page, not per page or per traffic.

**Sporadic Influencer and Event Marketing**

Brands get flooded constantly with inbox messages: sponsoring events, influencer birthday parties, celebrity charity dinners. Then there are DMs from every possible UGC creator and cold outreach from influencers.

The problem is sometimes you get excited and spend $2,000 here, $7,000 here, $10,000 there. Suddenly you're out $200,000 with nothing attributable to show. Sure, you might get content back, but did that content drive equivalent profit or even brand awareness? Probably not.

You need a proper strategy around influencers and events. Don't spend just to spend or participate. These opportunities exist 24/7 and never go away. It's not the last dinner that will happen.

For consumable products, most people reaching out likely just want your product. Offer them: "Instead of sponsoring with $10,000 plus product, we'll just give you product in exchange for a couple of social tags and content." Most say yes because of course they want product and can probably get sponsorship elsewhere.

**Non-Net New Customer Acquisition**

Many brands mess this up, starting with ASO. They don't put a 0% existing customer cap, leaving it at 10-30%. Don't do this if focusing on net new customers, which you should for largest campaigns.

Applause is similar. If you look at incrementality studies, many running app loving campaigns don't have exclusions, so you're not always acquiring net new customers. You get reorders, which works for highly consumable or repeat purchase products, but otherwise might not be right for you.

Make sure any channels, campaigns, and platforms focus on net new customer acquisition.

**Paying PR Agencies Egregious Amounts of Money**

I don't know why PR agencies set standards of $20,000-30,000 retainers to get written up. These agencies have relationships with editors and leverage those to get your brand written up in publications.

The ridiculous retainer isn't worth it. That $25,000 would be better invested in a creator-focused agency getting you 500-1,000 videos lit up daily on TikTok, Reels, or YouTube Shorts. That drives more awareness than PR.

Yes, you might get a Forbes exclusive for your launch or Variety feature, but that article gets 472 views total. Meanwhile, 800 videos daily for the same $25,000 monthly retainer gets 472 views per hour per video. It's not worth it.

Instead, work with creator agencies or put that budget toward getting eyeballs with creators. If you want PR logos, go buy sponsored articles. Press Cart and similar sites let you buy sponsored articles for flat fees. A Vogue Australia article might cost $1,500; GQ United States might be $3,700. You pay once, get your article and logo, and move on.

People don't even trust that stuff as much anymore anyway. They want to know that real customers and random creators on TikTok are using products, enjoying them, and that products live up to promises.

## Underrated Tactics

**Increasing Opt-In Rates for Email and SMS**

This is such easy, overnight revenue growth. Most brands opt in at about 3-7%. That's way too low.

Use two tools: Black Crow and Alia. Black Crow understands user behavior and patterns to predict if you'll be a high LTV customer, understand what you're selling, or need education. It decides when to serve a pop-up based on initial site interactions. Alia is the actual pop-up. Combined, you can usually get 15% email opt-in rates.

Think about the difference between 7% and 15%. That's 8% of traffic not being collected for welcome flows and revenue. That's low-hanging revenue sitting there waiting for you.

**Collecting and Syndicating Product Reviews**

Product reviews have been put on the back burner by many brands. I don't understand why. While creator reviews and everyday people unboxing on TikTok are the most authentic, you shouldn't ignore collecting reviews.

The vanity metric of review count is huge for social proof and conversion. If you get few reviews per 100 purchases, you're not doing enough. Don't just use Okendo's template emails. Customize messaging to feel on-brand, like a small business asking customers for a favor. Make the process frictionless and easy. Don't make your grandma struggle to write reviews on her iPhone.

Then syndicate reviews everywhere: Target, Nordstrom, TikTok Shop, Shopify's Shop app. Distribute reviews across channels because one place won't get them all. Collectively, 5,000 reviews across platforms is much stronger than 500 reviews for conversion.

**Responding to All Ad Comments**

This one is huge. I used to personally respond to every Facebook and Instagram ad comment every day after everyone left.

Always respond to every comment, even positive ones. An emoji response from the brand works. If someone asks a question, most others probably have it too. Respond and link to a product page or blog post that explains it. Use vanity URLs so links aren't clunky.

Instead of nick.co/product/cookbook?utm_source=meta_comments, use nick.co/cookbook. As you scale ads and get more comments daily, hire someone from Oceans to sit in Business Manager and respond. I believe responding through Business Manager performs better than using third-party tools built on APIs. I don't have proof, but after ten years of running media, I've consistently seen better performance within Business Manager itself.

**Optimizing All PDP Experiences, Even Outside Shopify**

Don't just focus on your own website's product pages. Optimize product pages everywhere you sell: Shop app, TikTok Shop, Meta Shops, Goop, Revolve, Amazon.

On Amazon, create Amazon-specific assets. Look at best-selling brands and copy them. On TikTok Shop, you have three places to play: carousel images (treat as a landing page with each section), product title, and description with text and imagery (also like a landing page).

On Nordstrom, Revolve, or Goop, if you don't have the right information or product copy, fix it. That reflects your brand on every marketplace, not just the marketplace itself. Maximize conversion rates because that feeds into brand equity, experience, and ultimately purchase orders.
