# Limited Supply: Working with Influencers and What Makes Brands Investable

As a brand, you're spending significant dollars driving traffic to your site, but ninety-eight percent of that traffic is anonymous. Once visitors leave, it becomes difficult to find them again. This is where understanding how to work strategically with influencers and build sustainable business models becomes critical.

## Working with Influencers: A Strategic Framework

When considering influencer partnerships, there are four primary reasons to engage with them. The first is content creation. These are individuals who excel at producing high-quality content, though they may not have massive followings. You can identify them through their work on platforms like TikTok, Instagram, YouTube, or Pinterest.

The second reason is reach. You're looking for individuals with substantial followings and ideally high engagement rates. The specific niche doesn't matter as much as their ability to reach large audiences quickly. Think of this tier as encompassing influencers with millions of followers who can simply expose your product to many people.

The third reason is alignment and co-sign. This is where you find a wellness influencer or someone like Andrew Huberman who can authentically recommend your product. The value here comes from the specific endorsement of someone whose opinion carries weight in a particular space. Testing has shown that placing quotes from these types of influencers on product pages significantly increases conversion rates compared to other forms of social proof.

The fourth reason is whitelisting. This involves taking content created by someone and running it as an advertisement through their account handle. This doesn't necessarily require the person to be an influencer in the traditional sense—it could be a meme page, publisher page, or any account that provides credibility through its existing audience.

When searching for content creators, TikTok and YouTube are the best platforms. YouTube creators have typically developed strong skills in speaking directly to the camera and storytelling since the platform requires these abilities to build any substantial following. Vloggers in particular excel at creating a sense of one-on-one conversation with viewers.

TikTok is excellent for finding creators within specific niches who may have limited followings but demonstrate genuine expertise. For example, searching for terms like "beverage commercial setup" might reveal creators with only a few hundred followers who are exceptionally skilled at creating the exact content you need. These creators often charge reasonable rates precisely because they don't have large followings.

When working with creators, prioritize platforms that enable social shopping, including Instagram, YouTube, and TikTok. This allows creators to tag your products, which adds credibility to your product listings and contributes to better performance in social shopping catalogs and shop pages.

Most brands work with influencers on either seeding or reach basis, but a better approach is finding content creators to retain on ongoing contracts. Rather than paying for one-off posts, consider retaining creators at monthly rates like twenty-five hundred to thirty-five hundred dollars in exchange for four to six videos or two to three videos plus ten to fifteen images you can use across email and Instagram.

Ongoing retainers offer multiple advantages. They help diversify your content so nothing feels stale or monotonous. More importantly, creators on the front lines hear constantly from their followers about what resonates and what doesn't. They can modify content based on real feedback. Sephora does this well by conducting quarterly check-ins with influencers to understand emerging trends and product preferences their audiences are discussing.

Regarding compensation structure, you can approach whitelisting in different ways. Some brands pay a flat fee, which might range from two hundred fifty to five hundred dollars monthly for smaller creators up to five thousand to ten thousand dollars monthly for major celebrities. Alternatively, you can align incentives by offering one to two percent of ad spend as compensation for content you successfully scale.

When briefing creators, the ideal approach depends on their familiarity with your product. If they regularly consume your product and know it well, have them develop their own brief and then review it for accuracy and compliance. If they're less familiar, let them try the product first and then provide a rough brief based on what works well in paid and organic channels. They can then finalize the content in their voice.

For tight timelines, provide structured briefs broken into components rather than a single document. Instead of one lengthy script, create seven different hooks tested from paid channels, seven main talking points, and five to six call-to-actions. This approach generates approximately twenty reusable clips that can be edited and combined in different ways.

Ensure you receive raw clips in a Google Drive folder rather than finished videos. This allows you to cut, splice, and isolate narration from music and transitions. You need the ability to edit in-house and test different combinations of hooks, talking points, and calls-to-action.

Finally, create dedicated landing pages and offers for influencer campaigns. If someone gets excited by an influencer's message and clicks the ad, they shouldn't arrive at a generic homepage. Instead, direct them to a page featuring the influencer's picks, a special offer, and additional information for someone who's interested but not yet ready to purchase.

## What Makes a Brand Investable

The first quality of an investable consumer brand is differentiation that doesn't require extra cost. This might be a built-in audience through celebrity involvement, manufacturing advantages like owning a production facility that provides cost benefits, or existing relationships in major retail channels. David Protein's protein technology is an example—it provides differentiation without adding cost.

Innovation represents the second critical factor. This could be proprietary technology exclusive to your brand, like an ingredient others can't access, or a patented formulation competitors can't replicate. Examples include K18's innovative shampoo compound, Bonafide Health's patented supplement formulations, and even something as straightforward as HexClad's hexagonal pan pattern. Innovation helps cut through market noise and justifies premium positioning.

Third, the team must understand core business metrics. Red flags include projections of seven to ten dollar customer acquisition costs that will decline over time, or claims that customer acquisition costs will significantly decrease as you scale. These statements suggest founders haven't actually run performance marketing campaigns. While some brands achieve these metrics, they typically balance them with substantial organic revenue. Most founders presenting these numbers lack real paid advertising experience.

Fourth, build a platform rather than just a single product. Lalo exemplifies this by creating a suite of products that appeal to parents—once a customer buys one product, they can purchase complementary items. The exception is subscription businesses with clear problem-solution fits, like AG1 or wellness subscription services. Single-purchase products like weighted blankets represent poor investment opportunities because customers typically buy once and never return.

Fifth, create multiple revenue streams. Subscription models work exceptionally well alongside hardware—air purifiers with filter subscriptions, shower heads with filter subscriptions, or smart mattresses with monthly app fees. These businesses develop higher lifetime value and demonstrate understanding of how to create recurring relationships with customers.

Sixth, ensure the product is demonstrable with clear problem and solution messaging. Many first-time founders miss this because they haven't yet experienced market rejection. Products should obviously improve life in measurable ways. Brands that require education tend to face longer sales cycles.

Seventh, the product should be a priority purchase rather than something customers cancel during financial hardship. Colostrum, filtered water, and pet food subscriptions retain customers through difficult times. Products that are likely to be canceled when finances tighten present future challenges.

Eighth, develop multi-beneficial products. Supplements with multiple benefits, low-carb high-protein foods, or beverages that are both flavorful and have no sugar create higher lifetime value and can appeal to different customer segments based on different benefits.

Finally, maintain a reasonable valuation. Twenty million dollar valuations for pre-revenue brands are no longer viable. A realistic approach involves raising a small pre-seed to test and validate, then potentially raising another extension or a proper seed round based on results.

## What Makes SaaS Investable

For software companies, the first requirement is solving a top-five founder problem. This might be understanding financial numbers, keeping subscribers engaged, sending revenue-generating emails, or reducing churn. If your software doesn't address critical founder challenges, sales cycles will be difficult and you'll be first on the expense-cutting list.

Second, the software must contribute directly to brand KPIs required for growth or fundraising. Companies like Finaloop, Iris, and ShipStation help maintain infrastructure that allows scaling. A random SMS addition with one feature charging usage-based fees will struggle to compete.

Third, investable software companies employ multi-prong approaches to create lock-in. Rebuy offers forty-seven products within their platform—from reorders to smart upsells in carts to AI-based PDP upsells. This allows brands to test the product in various ways and lets the software company land and expand across the customer.

Fourth, the software must be easily set up and drive clearly attributable incremental revenue or profit. Site speed optimization tools that don't directly show revenue impact will eventually be discontinued because founders can't see ROI.

Fifth, proprietary technology and benefits are essential. Many AI-based platforms are simply wrappers around third-party APIs from OpenAI, Perplexity, or Google. They're combining existing tools rather than creating something genuinely new. While there may be enterprise value in integration, these lack the proprietary advantage needed for investment.

Finally, maintain reasonable valuations. Software companies shouldn't command one hundred dollars in valuation per dollar of revenue. For a company raising two million dollars at a twenty million dollar valuation, they must generate meaningful ARR to raise at a higher valuation next round. If they spend two million and only generate two hundred thousand in annual recurring revenue, they'll face a down round that directly impacts your investment.
