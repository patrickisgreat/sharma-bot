# Limited Supply: Black Friday BFCM Recap with Simon Wool

Welcome back to Limited Supply. Today's episode is a candid conversation with Simon Wool from Wool Ventures, a growth consulting firm that works with brands in the one to ten million and one to twenty million revenue range. We sat down to pick his brain on Black Friday performance, what worked better than last year, and what to expect going forward. We talked about changing sales mid-sale, the lull before Black Friday and between Black Friday and Cyber Monday, and strategies for Cyber Monday. We also drifted into conversations about new channels that are working well and some interesting insights about cannabis brands.

## Black Friday Performance and Consumer Behavior

What was immediately clear from looking at Black Friday this year is that brands went live with sales really early. Years ago, the strategy was no sale until Black Friday, then a huge sale on the day itself carrying through the weekend, with an even bigger sale on Cyber Monday. This taught consumers to wait. This year, brands started sales as early as November first with the same offers continuing throughout the entire Black Friday season.

Looking at the data across a portfolio of brands, there was a massive spike in shopper intent from November first through the seventh. Then it went completely flat from the seventh through the twentieth. Things picked back up on the twentieth, with spikes continuing through the end of Black Friday. Consumers have learned that November twentieth through Black Friday is when the consistent deals are, and Cyber Monday carries another shift in the offer.

The brand that performed best did something different. They kept twenty percent off from the twenty-first through Thanksgiving. Then on Thanksgiving at four or five PM, they increased the offer to thirty-five percent off site-wide. This was their Black Friday offer, and it went live Thursday evening. That momentum carried all the way through Friday, making it the best day of the year. Saturday saw a lull, but Sunday spiked back up with a different offer: they maintained the thirty-five percent but marked down certain styles on the site heavily. This is doorbusters, essentially increasing what the sale actually was. The momentum carried strongly into Monday.

Across the board, November was a strong month. Year-over-year numbers are up for most brands, but this is due partly to sales starting early. The key insight is that while Black Friday generated massive revenue, it was softer as a percentage of total November revenue than in previous years because sales had already started cannibalizing some of that demand.

## Email and Text Strategy During BFCM

Most brands had three emails scheduled on Black Friday itself, not counting resends. The typical pattern was an email at midnight with a resend at seven AM, another email at ten AM with a resend at one PM, then another at four PM with a resend at seven PM. While this might generate spam complaints, the reality is that people's inboxes are so flooded during Black Friday and Cyber Monday that if the email drives incremental revenue, it's working. There should be no real limit to how many emails you send during this period.

Some brands went as far as six to eight emails on Black Friday alone, which is definitely up year-over-year. Plain text emails and letters from the founder are also great tactics during this period. The efficiency mindset should prevail: you want to spend as much as possible while driving revenue and converting as many people as possible. Every other part of the year might focus on acquiring new customers, but during Black Friday and Cyber Monday, it's about maximizing revenue from your existing base while also acquiring new customers opportunistically.

CB2 was particularly aggressive with text messaging, sending an estimated fifty text messages from Thanksgiving day onward. These included videos and images, which are expensive to send, but the investment seems to have paid off. The general principle holds: most brands don't send enough emails, though some might send too many texts.

## Cyber Monday Strategy

Cyber Monday looked different than Black Friday. The day itself looked relatively flat, like a normal day, until three or four PM when it shot up massively. This makes sense because nobody goes shopping in stores on Cyber Monday like they do on Black Friday. Everyone is at work, but online shopping is convenient, so that's when conversion happens.

The best email sends on Cyber Monday timed around NFL football games. When people are sitting on their couch watching TV and get an email from a brand they want to convert from, and there's a good enough sale, they convert. Emails sent around eight-thirty to nine PM Eastern time converted best during the games.

Email volume was particularly high on Cyber Monday. The key differentiator was scarcity messaging, last-chance messaging, countdown timers on emails and websites, and final markdowns or doorbusters to clear out old inventory. One interesting observation was that most people who went shopping during Black Friday went to physical stores. Cyber Monday is primarily an online shopping event because people are working but can shop online.

## Post-BFCM Retention Strategy

For retaining customers acquired during Black Friday, several specific tactics work well. Handwritten mailers thanking customers for their purchase and emphasizing support for a small business over big box retailers create strong emotional connections. On Tuesday when you shut off the sale, still honor it if people email you. The promo code stays technically live, but you're not advertising it anymore. This creates customers for life.

The first marketing email post-purchase should be a letter from the founder, welcoming them to the family and thanking them for buying. It doesn't matter who it's from in the header; it can come from a general email address, but it should feel personal and authentic. The founder should share how they would use the product and invite questions.

Post-purchase upsells marketed as Black Friday offers perform ten times better than normal post-purchase upsells. If you forget to turn off the copy change after Black Friday, it actually continues to work that well throughout the rest of the year, so leaving it on can be a smart decision.

It's important to understand that Black Friday customers are not going to be as good as customers acquired at other times of year. A customer acquired in September with no discount will be better than a Black Friday shopper. But that doesn't mean Black Friday customers are bad; they're still valuable. The point is not to focus on new customer acquisition during Black Friday. You've already built your top of funnel. Black Friday is about converting those people and maximizing overall revenue and blended ROAS at the most efficient rate possible.

There's also good carryover from Black Friday bargain hunters into retail and Amazon sales. People who try your product at half off might not buy from you directly again, but they'll purchase at Target or Walmart when they're on a regular shopping trip. The brand gets a halo effect even if the direct customer isn't returning.

## New Channels: Zagged and App Loving

One underground channel that's working exceptionally well is called Zagged, which uses clipping technology. This involves posting slideshow-style ads on social platforms where someone tells a story about how they had a problem and solved it with a product. It looks like organic content, not a traditional ad. The platform offers super cheap CPMs at around three to three-fifty per thousand views with guaranteed views and real engagement.

One brand running this strategy saw absolute virality, with organic traffic and direct traffic increasing significantly. Brands running this see about twenty-five to thirty percent lower CPAs than brands not running it. You're getting massive top-of-funnel audience building over time. The posts generate real engagement from real people commenting about trying the product, not bot comments or bought views.

The channel has no category restrictions either. For cannabis brands, because this is technically organic content being posted, they can talk about products pretty openly. If one account gets taken down for content reasons, they just spin another one back up. Unlike ad accounts, there's no compliance issue with that approach.

App Loving has been another strong performer, particularly for supplement and cannabis brands. It was reopened starting October first after being in beta. CPMs are good, ROAS is really high, and the customer quality seems strong across the board. However, some preliminary incrementality studies suggest returning customers make up a significant portion of conversions, so the channel works better once you exclude returning customers. Some brands report twenty-five to forty percent of their Black Friday customers came from App Loving. As the platform evolves and adds exclusion options, it should become even more powerful.

## Ad Creative and Messaging

The ads that performed best during Black Friday and Cyber Monday were straightforward. They weren't trying to walk people through why they should buy the product. They were business-as-usual static ads with the offer front and center. Don't do something crazy with a thumb stop; just sell the product. This isn't a Facebook ad trying to build desire; it's a sales ad.

Similarly, with email subject lines, emails saying something is "up to sixty percent off" don't perform as well as emails saying "thirty percent off site-wide." Up to sixty percent makes people think most items are barely discounted. A clear percentage off applies to everything and triggers immediate purchase intent.

Memorable ads are the ones that have a sale front and center and make you laugh. One brand posted a sticky note graphic that just said thirty-five percent off. It was super simple, right to the point, and it sells. The principle is KISS: keep it simple, stupid.

## Strategy Going Forward

The biggest tactical recommendation for next year is to build your strategy around consumer behavior. Start your sale the first week of November with an early Black Friday offer. Then pull back on ad spend from the seventeenth through the twenty-first to improve efficiency while conserving budget. This lull is real and expected.

Launch your Thanksgiving offer on the twenty-first through Thanksgiving itself. Then on Thanksgiving, increase the offer and launch new ads built specifically for that higher offer. This becomes your Black Friday deal. Run it through the beginning of Cyber Monday. On Cyber Monday, launch new ads built for a Cyber Monday-specific offer.

The critical point here is don't turn off your old ads. If your ads have been working for a week with a twenty percent off message and someone sees them and gets to your site where it now says thirty percent off, you still win. The old ads have great click-through rates and convert well. There's no reason to turn them off just because you have new ads. Your site's onsite offer will convert them at the higher discount regardless.

Make sure the onsite offer is crystal clear. Put it in the cart, on the product detail page, in the hero section, in the top navigation banner. If you create a funnel where someone can click on the wrong promo code, get to your site, and check out, you're doing it right. That's the truth.

Don't spend money on multiple sales during the middle of November. That's just going to hurt yourself and create stress. Build your funnel at the beginning of Q1, then sell that funnel and make as much money as possible during Black Friday and Cyber Monday. This is the time to do it. The end of December into January is another time to capitalize, but Black Friday is the biggest opportunity of the year.

Send more emails at the right times, use SMS strategically, and make it fun. Put your sale front and center and make people remember you. The simplest, most direct approach wins.
