## Testing Marketer Skills in the Era of Data Expertise

When hiring marketers, context is everything. The term "marketer" is too broad—you need to understand what specific type you're hiring: a growth marketer, performance marketer, brand marketer, or someone to execute the founder's vision. Look for patterns in candidates' backgrounds and ensure their experience aligns with your brand's needs.

A critical mistake is hiring someone based solely on prestigious past employers without considering the fundamental differences in their previous roles. For example, a marketer who sold high-AOV cookware at a well-known brand may struggle selling a $89 hardware product with a recurring subscription model. The economics and marketing approaches are entirely different. Most marketers can't flex between drastically different product categories—that's rare talent.

The fundamentals matter: analytics, copywriting, media buying, Shopify knowledge, merchandising, and offers. But great marketers also need an X factor—that extra edge, vision, and fire that takes brands to the next level. Look for curiosity and scrappiness.

Red flags include people who take excessive credit for work they didn't do. The best approach is asking "tell me about a time" questions to understand their actual experiences and see if they give credit to their team. Real experience matters more than claims.

Best talent isn't looking for jobs. Don't expect to find them through LinkedIn easy apply. Instead, find people at similar organizations who haven't been promoted recently—those are likely to jump. You have to actively recruit rather than passively wait for applications.

## Maximizing AI for Selling

There are four waves of AI adoption. The first wave was "anonymous Indians"—basically if-then statements and data sent offshore for processing. We're finishing the second wave now: mass consumer AI like ChatGPT, Perplexity, Claude, and Google Gemini that anyone can use for questions and document uploads.

The third wave, currently beginning, is SaaS AI—companies incorporating AI into their platforms to learn, research, analyze, make recommendations, understand patterns, and produce briefs and reports. This is where AI agents come in. Rather than replacing individual tasks, an AI agent acts like a team captain with multiple agents underneath handling different tasks simultaneously.

The fourth wave will be open source AI, where individuals and creators build custom AI tools they can lease or sell. Imagine a storyteller creating an AI that helps write better hooks and angles for stories.

For e-commerce specifically, start with Triple Whale, Motion, or ChatGPT Pro. Triple Whale is particularly effective because it excels at organizing and segmenting data from Shopify, Klaviyo, store reports, organic social, Amazon, etc. AI can't help you if your inputs are messy. Clean, organized, segmented data is crucial—bad inputs lead to bad outputs.

AI is already here and replacing existing employees. Companies are doing this because there's no reason to add overhead if you don't need it. Your job today is 300% harder because AI has advanced so much, and many jobs can now be replaced by AI agents.

## Static and Image Ads Without Video

Many brands are profitably using only static or image ads well into significant revenue. The issue is you're probably only looking at mega-brands like Ridge, Jones Road, Grooms, Caraway, and Hexclad. Brands doing $5 to $30 million often run hundreds of statics exclusively.

Many brands now use AI for video creation with tools like Veo, which makes eight-second clips. They also use UGC from creators and commissioned content. The best approach is often making content yourself—a Sony camera and a white wall in your office can produce ads in 30 minutes.

Statics have a lower CPM starting point, giving them a better shot. Focus on two things: whether your brand is well-received when seen, and whether the creative is good enough to earn a click. You don't need to sell the product in the ad or get the conversion—just get the click.

Analyze your Facebook ads manager funnel metrics: CPM, frequency, click-through rate, cost per click, view content cost, add to cart cost, checkout cost, and purchase cost. This clearly shows where drop-off occurs. High CPM means poor audience alignment. High CTR with low CPM means your targeting is right but the ad isn't compelling. High CTR and low CPC but low add-to-cart means your site messaging is weak.

The problem might not be your static ads—it could be a poor product page, weak checkout messaging, or misalignment between ad copy and landing page promises. Identify the specific bottleneck in your funnel and fix that.

## Influencer Strategy for $20-$50M Brands

For brands in this revenue range, several influencer strategies work well. YouTube organic sponsorships involve paying for placements in videos from popular creators where they mention your brand at the video start and in the description. With a good consultant or agency relationship, you see results within two weeks. The best part is that evergreen content continues generating value for years afterward.

Whitelisting authentic content is easy and effective. Send creators your product, ensure they actually like it, then run ads using their authentic content. It works better than obvious celebrity placements because it feels genuine.

Product seeding to affiliates and creators works well. Ask them to post content, then put ad spend behind content that significantly outperforms organic baselines. You can share ad spend revenue with the creator as compensation for using their content.

Dedicated merchandising pages with custom landing pages, bundles, offers, and unique coupon codes tied to influencers go a long way with their audiences. Include the influencer's name and explain the authentic connection between them and your brand.

Co-branded products are possible but might be better saved until you're larger and your core products are established. Advertorials through influencer blogs or platforms like Grapevine, which has 3,000+ creators with websites and social media, offer another option. Affiliate programs through platforms like TikTok-based social tools provide additional leverage.
