## Client Tactics

For an apparel brand we're working with on 2023 planning, here are some tactics to test that ninety-nine percent of brands don't actually implement.

**Dynamic Product Ads (DPA)**

DPA ads match products to users based on what Facebook knows about them and their site interactions. You upload all your products at once—say, two thousand t-shirts—and Facebook decides which one is optimal for each person. You're tapping into a different engine than normal ads, and performance is usually completely different.

The challenge is keeping catalogs updated, especially for large brands. There are service businesses that charge monthly retainers to maintain catalogs. That's actually an opportunity—you could make easy ten thousand a month maintaining catalogs for stores doing a million dollars a month.

The issue is that DPA pulls product images from your product detail page, which is usually just the product on a white background. For enriched catalogs, use a company like Marpipe. You upload your catalog and use an editor like Canva to add elements. You can put the product subtitle in the top left, price in the bottom right, and use rules like "if the product costs over thirty-five dollars, display the Afterpay logo." You create fully optimized creative for each product rather than just plain product photos. Click-through rates go way up and conversions increase significantly.

**Sponsored Editorial Content**

Create different stories and run them to different audiences. A mom buys the product for one reason, a Gen Z person for another, and someone in Miami for yet another. Create content and run it to specific audiences instead of always using evergreen ads.

**Whitelisting Influencers**

Create content with influencers and run it as ads. You give them an authorization code that populates the creative in the ad. You can shut it off anytime or revoke access.

**TikTok Spark Ads**

This brand has customers who do hauls with their clothes—try-on hauls where someone goes shopping, gets a bunch of stuff, and creates a video wearing all the outfits. You can go to customers who've already done this and offer them a thousand dollars to turn their haul into an ad. You just need their authorization code.

**Polygon Audiences**

Agile has a platform called Polygon where you can create custom audiences. For example, you can target everyone who's visited all the national Target stores twice a month. You can then build a lookalike audience off that.

**Credit Card Offers**

Companies like Cardlytics work with Chase Bank to put offers on credit cards. You can offer twenty percent off to their Sapphire Reserve holders and ten percent off to other cardholders. You pay for the discount and the affiliate fee, and you're competing against some of the biggest direct response advertisers, so you have to give up a good amount.

**RewardStyle**

RewardStyle is probably the one affiliate network you can always rely on to move revenue. It was started by Amber, who was a blogger frustrated with how hard it was to monetize outfit posts on Instagram. She created an app called Like to Know It where she could link her outfit items and earn commission. She built a massive database of fashion and lifestyle creators. The advertiser side requires application and includes big brands like Kate Spade and Everlane. Unlike ShareASale, where scammers apply and immediately run Google ads against you stealing your traffic, RewardStyle is legitimate.
