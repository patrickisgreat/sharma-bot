# Limited Supply: Building Products Founders Wish Existed

This episode features Isaac, founder of Mini Katana and Kanpai Foods, discussing product ideas and infrastructure gaps that should exist in the consumer brand ecosystem. They explore both retail and digital challenges facing modern brands.

## The TikTok Situation and Geopolitical Context

Isaac emphasizes that savvy brands saw the TikTok challenge coming due to mounting geopolitical tensions and China-based business risks. The conversation traces back to Trump's first presidency when he attempted to ban TikTok. Isaac frames TikTok as brain slop disguised as a psyop, comparing it to an absurd Cold War scenario where the Soviet Union owned Fox News. The point is that during a new Cold War, allowing a foreign adversary to own a major American media company seems inconceivable yet has been normalized.

Despite these concerns, Nik acknowledges missing TikTok and credits the platform with his own existence. Isaac notes that after Mini Katana accumulated hundreds of millions of views, major competitors like Skittles and Mars launched their own freeze-dried candy lines. Rather than cannibalizing sales, this validation legitimized the entire category and increased velocity for Kanpai, a phenomenon known as tailwinds from category legitimation.

## Attribution and Data Infrastructure for Retail

Isaac identifies a critical gap: there's no meaningful way to track how meta spend impacts retail velocities at target retailers. While DDC brands have tools like Triple Whale and North Beam, retail multi-channel attribution essentially doesn't exist. The fundamental challenge is that retailers don't provide real-time data—Kanpai gets monthly reports, and Isaac manually negotiated a $40,000-per-year data arrangement with their broker to see competitor benchmarks.

Data brokers like Spins obtain scanner data, but individual-item-level data remains inaccessible. This represents a billion-dollar opportunity given retail's significantly larger GMV compared to DDC. A comprehensive retail media attribution platform would solve a massive problem for brands scaling through traditional retail channels.

## Landing Pages Connected to Retail Data

Building on the data infrastructure gap, Nik proposes a landing page optimization tool tied to retailer sell-through data. The concept involves running performance marketing ads to a landing page that locates the nearest Target store. Then, within seven to thirty days, brands could track whether users actually purchased the product at retail. This requires solving the underlying retailer data access problem but would enable meaningful performance marketing attribution.

A simpler iteration could notify users whenever products go on promo, pushing them toward trial during promotional periods—a critical lever for brand growth. Novel wallet pass technology already enables geofenced notifications when users approach retail locations, though the concern about attracting deal-seeking consumers who only repeat on promotion remains valid.

## Guerrilla Sampling at Scale

Isaac discusses their Cybertruck sampling strategy. Since brands can't properly sample at Target without using Advantage, their in-house agency that often underperforms, Kanpai wraps a Cybertruck and samples in Target parking lots without permission. This generates nearly equivalent outcomes to in-store sampling without the overhead. There should be an agency that scales this nationally, charging hourly rates for on-ground sampling teams. Red Bull's iconic sampling campaigns demonstrate that large-scale guerrilla sampling drives category awareness and brand awareness simultaneously.

## Centralized Content Management for Social Commerce

Using platforms like Contentful or Salsify as reference points, Nik identifies that while DDC brands can update product information once and distribute across sites, social commerce platforms lack this capability. Currently, brands must separately update TikTok Shop, Instagram Shops, Meta Shops, and YouTube, creating massive friction especially for brands with large catalogs or lean teams. A platform that syndicates products, pricing, flash sales, and reviews across all social shopping channels would massively reduce operational burden.

Isaac pushes back somewhat, noting that channels worth maintaining deserve focused attention. However, for large catalogs or lean teams, this centralization would unlock efficiency gains. The concept could extend to international platforms like Taobao, Temu, and regional variations, addressing a genuine pain point across the social commerce ecosystem.

## AI-Powered Voice Translation for Ads

Isaac shares that 11 labs technology allows training AI on a person's voice and translating it to any language while maintaining the exact same voice and accent. They tested this for their Spanish Mini Katana channel, which has six million subscribers. No equivalent service exists for translated ad campaigns, which represents a missed opportunity for international expansion.

The idea is a specialized creative production service that automatically translates Meta ads into multiple languages using the same voice and creative elements, then manages campaigns across international markets. This eliminates the need for local influencers or creators while maintaining consistency. It would function similarly to a software product where brands input their ad account, creative gets translated, and campaigns launch internationally or to Spanish-speaking domestic markets.

## Website Translation with AI

Nik extends this concept to Shopify websites. When they explored translating their site last year, no good AI solutions existed, but recent advances make this viable. An AI system could translate all website text across pages while allowing human review and approval. This would enable rapid international expansion without the traditional localization bottleneck. The key challenge remains context—jokes, puns, and cultural nuances can get lost in translation—but current AI capabilities are increasingly sophisticated enough to handle this.

## M&A Due Diligence Tool

High Beam and other roll-up platforms should build an M&A analysis tool that connects into Shopify, Facebook, Klaviyo, and ERP systems to run preliminary analysis on acquisition targets. The tool would verify customer legitimacy through TransUnion APIs, calculate true LTV, CAC, profit margins, and inventory costs, then provide a preliminary recommendation on deal viability.

Founders frequently face the problem that M&A markets are opaque. They don't know when their brand is acquisition-ready or what makes a good target. A tool that combines uploaded sales data, customer lists, and automated financial analysis could help founders understand their own valuation and give acquirers preliminary signals about deal quality before engaging bankers. This would accelerate due diligence and help founders understand if pursuing exit is worthwhile.

## Automated Retail Media Campaign Management

Retail media spending is straightforward but fragmented. Kanpai runs campaigns on Target, Walmart, Instacart, and DoorDash, but there's no unified dashboard for optimization or management. An AI-powered tool similar to Espresso for DDC but designed for retail media would automatically manage campaigns across retailers, consolidate reporting, and intelligently allocate budgets based on performance and promotional calendars.

For example, if running a Target promo with Circle members only, the system could automatically allocate $5,000 daily to drive that specific promotion while tracking it against all other active promotions. This is simple enough that AI agents could manage it by reading screens and APIs, yet no comprehensive solution exists.

## Supplement and Food Safety Testing

Third-party lab testing and transparency represent an emerging consumer concern. A company called Val on Twitter calls out supplement brands making false claims. Cure Hydration already displays third-party lab test results on their website through a widget. An infrastructure company could streamline batch testing where brands send production batches to labs and results automatically update on their website. Given consumer obsession with health and safety, this could become table stakes for brands competing in health-adjacent categories.

## Better Referral Programs

Current referral platforms like send-five-get-five force users to find unique URLs, share them, and wait for rewards. The experience is too transactional. Historical growth leaders like Uber and Dropbox succeeded because referrals were deeply native to the core product experience—one click within the app versus hunting for links.

A modern referral system would integrate into the mobile checkout experience as a shareable post-purchase extension. Instead of splitting rewards fifty-fifty, giving the full credit to the referred friend makes it feel more generous and less transactional. HelloFresh demonstrates this with gift cards included in packaging that can be handed to friends. A SaaS solution enabling this type of gifting and referral natively within the checkout experience would drive word-of-mouth more effectively than current platforms.

## Group Buying Platform

Nik proposes reimagining Groupon for modern e-commerce. Group buying works well for discounts—the larger the group, the deeper the discount. If ten friends commit to HelloFresh subscriptions together, they might receive 30% off for a year. At fifty friends, 50% off. This makes buying a social activity again, similar to how commerce functioned before e-commerce when shopping was inherently social.

Groupon failed partly because it attracted low-quality merchants and low-quality products. A modern version focusing on quality brands and leveraging social infrastructure could work. The platform would need to handle logistics like Goody, which simplifies corporate gifting by centralizing order placement and custom rates across retailers. Commerce used to be social; modern e-commerce can reclaim that through group buying mechanics.

## App Clips for Subscription and Post-Purchase

App Clips, announced by Apple in 2023, remain underutilized despite their potential. Unlike full apps, App Clips provide lightweight native experiences triggered by text or web links. Explode, a photo-sharing app, uses App Clips to create a Snapchat-like experience within iMessage. For subscription brands like Kanpai, App Clips could enable post-purchase engagement—customers receive a text saying their next box is shipping and tap an App Clip to select free samples for that month.

Most commerce platforms haven't integrated App Clip creation capabilities, but this represents an arbitrage opportunity. Early movers who understand and implement App Clips for common subscription and post-purchase use cases could establish significant advantages before the rest of the market adopts them. The technology has been available over a year, and nobody has capitalized on it at scale.

## Broader Themes

The conversation highlights that building consumer brands increasingly requires solving infrastructure and operational problems. Retail attribution, centralized content management, international expansion, and automation represent massive pain points that existing solutions inadequately address. Many of these gaps exist because the tools haven't caught up with reality, not because the problems are unsolvable. Early builders who address these operational inefficiencies across the brand ecosystem have significant opportunities ahead.
