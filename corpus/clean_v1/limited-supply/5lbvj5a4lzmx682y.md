# Limited Supply Episode 1, Season 8

The advertising landscape has fundamentally shifted. Facebook's Advantage Plus Shopping campaigns have eliminated traditional funnel marketing concepts. There's no longer a distinct top of funnel, middle of funnel, and bottom of funnel approach. Instead, brands now give Facebook creative and conversion objectives, and the platform determines who should see what. When testing this system, thirty to fifty percent of budget is being spent on existing customers, which many consider wasteful for customer acquisition campaigns.

The core problem with Advantage Plus Shopping is the loss of granular targeting and creative control. Brands can't create separate top-of-funnel awareness creative and bottom-of-funnel conversion creative anymore because anyone could see anything. The platform handles all targeting decisions. Additionally, crucial targeting capabilities have been removed. While location exclusions are now possible, gender targeting has disappeared entirely. For brands like Alo Yoga or Nike that have distinct men's and women's product lines, this creates a significant operational challenge with no clear solution.

Facebook's algorithm is sophisticated enough to interpret creative content and make assumptions about audience gender based on visual elements. The company allegedly reads creative and identifies characteristics like the appearance of men or women within the images, then distributes accordingly. This represents a shift toward creative-based targeting rather than interest-based or demographic targeting.

There's been significant volatility in Facebook advertising over the past thirty days, particularly in March. Multiple brands experienced unpredictable spending patterns—accounts accelerating spend during certain hours then not spending at all. A Chrome update likely impacted tracking and attribution across many accounts. Facebook's ad manager itself has become less reliable, frequently taking credit for conversions that never actually occurred according to Shopify data. While some advertisers report the best results ever, others describe March as the worst month on record. Facebook representatives acknowledge experiencing multiple service outages that may have contributed to the instability.

The pixel's effectiveness has degraded significantly since the Apple privacy update. Prior to iOS 14, Facebook received vastly more data to optimize campaign performance. Today's pixel is substantially weaker. The solution has been adoption of third-party tools like Blodout, Elevr, and Black Crow to improve pixel functionality and data passing.

Interest-based targeting is being phased out completely within the next twelve to eighteen months, though other forms of targeting will remain. Advertisers will have the ability to overbid or underbid on certain parameters, but won't be able to select specific interests. This creates challenges for niche product categories like religious items or culturally specific products.

Video content now dominates successful Facebook advertising. The days of finding cheap stock photos on Google Images or Getty are gone. Successful brands are creating genuine, original, high-quality video content that's entertaining, bright, and visually appealing. Generic studio photography no longer cuts it—brands must be skilled content creators.

Creative warming has become a critical strategy that wasn't emphasized in previous years. The process involves testing creatives at lower budgets in separate campaigns, then pulling the best performers into larger-budget campaigns using the ad ID. This prevents Facebook from having to optimize between many new creatives simultaneously. The practice reflects a deeper understanding that Facebook needs learning data before scaling, though this approach is relatively new to the mainstream conversation.

Attribution windows matter, with seven-day click conversions outperforming seven-day click plus one-day view attribution windows according to some practitioners, though results appear category-dependent.

Facebook Shops are growing and generating significant revenue for some brands. At least one brand is generating one million dollars monthly through Facebook Shops, and some brands report twenty percent of revenue coming from this channel. The platform provides a trust layer that direct-to-consumer websites lack. Consumers are more confident purchasing through Facebook Shops because Facebook acts as an intermediary guaranteeing shipping performance and order accuracy.

The future will include influencer tagging capabilities within Facebook Shops during the second half of this year, with affiliate program integration likely following. This represents Facebook's competitive response to TikTok Shop. Eventually, Facebook may reduce incentives for driving traffic to external Shopify sites by charging a premium compared to Facebook Shops, effectively making Shopify's position more precarious. If Facebook continues improving its shop experience while potentially taking a percentage cut from direct Shopify traffic through premium advertising costs, the long-term viability of independent e-commerce platforms becomes questionable.

Hiring for Facebook ad expertise requires evaluating specific competencies. Candidates should have spent at least 1.5 times your planned monthly budget previously to understand the operational complexity. Someone comfortable with thirty thousand monthly should not be trusted with hundred-thousand budgets. The person should articulate a clear creative strategy and demonstrate deep funnel thinking beyond awareness and website clicks. A live audit of an actual account often reveals whether someone understands building middle-funnel audiences for longer sales cycles or is merely running bottom-funnel campaigns.

Poor audit performance typically involves misunderstanding campaign objectives. Running awareness campaigns or website click campaigns suggests following generic blog advice rather than real-world experience. For high-value products with extended decision cycles, conversion events should focus on micro-conversions like email capture or configuration customization rather than immediate sales. Some of the largest Facebook spenders operate at seven-million-dollar monthly budgets by leveraging website click campaigns effectively, understanding that conversion event spending caps out around one million monthly for most businesses.

When evaluating agencies, ask about clients where the relationship didn't work and understand why. Communication processes matter significantly depending on whether the agency handles creative production. Most agencies assign too many clients per team—healthy ratios appear to be three to four clients per team, though many agencies manage fifteen to thirty. Agencies must choose whether to compete on price, scale, or service quality. Most choose scale over service, resulting in degraded account management.

Outdoor Voices closed all twelve stores and is heading toward bankruptcy. The announcement was handled poorly, with employees being dismissed via message rather than individual conversations. The company had several strategic issues. In 2018, it was valued at one hundred ten million dollars but carried fifty-two weeks of inventory. In 2019, it spent twenty-five million on marketing, burned twenty million dollars, and achieved fifty million in revenue—a significant loss. At that time, half of revenue came from brick-and-mortar stores. The company raised twelve million dollars in 2020 at a thirty-million-dollar valuation with a two-times liquidation preference. It now appears entirely bankrupt.

The failure resulted from competing visions between venture capitalists, founder Ty Haney, and Mickey Drexler, who wanted physical stores. The brand created excellent products but failed to constantly introduce new merchandise as required in apparel. The company competes with Shein, Amazon Fashion, and fast fashion retailers requiring continuous product development cycles. Apparel is an exhausting business compared to consumable categories like personal care, which maintain steady demand without constant sourcing requirements.

Evaluating a distressed apparel business acquisition requires understanding what made it fail. Did stores destroy profitability, or are they profitable? Is there excessive dead inventory from three years ago? What happens to direct-to-consumer revenue if all stores close? Would you lose twenty-five percent of online sales due to regional store marketing effects? The reality is that multiple failed acquirers suggest the business carries bad luck. Victoria's Secret operates with a billion-four market cap on six billion in revenue, indicating the category's profitability challenges. Most investors should probably avoid apparel entirely.

There's a notable contrast in advertising spend between established automakers and Tesla. Tesla spent six point three million dollars in advertising in 2023 after barely advertising previously. General Motors spent three point six billion dollars. General Motors allocated five hundred eighty dollars in ad spend per vehicle sold, while Tesla essentially ran without paid advertising. This demonstrates how brand momentum and product desirability can eliminate traditional marketing requirements.

Semaglutide medications like Ozempic and Mounjaro represent an existential threat to entire industries. As these GLP-1 agonist drugs become more accessible, potentially in pill form, adoption could expand from the current top fifteen percent of the population significantly higher. The implications ripple across food companies like General Mills and Frito-Lay, fitness companies like Equinox, and other segments dependent on consumption-driven behavior. If fifty percent of the population takes semaglutide, the demand destruction for sugary foods and casual gym memberships becomes substantial. Eli Lilly now has a seven-hundred-billion-dollar market capitalization and is approaching one trillion dollars in value, driven primarily by these medications.

Entrepreneurs are building businesses around GLP-1 medications, and demand is essentially unlimited. Telehealth companies like Hims and Roman are positioned to capture significant market share, though larger pharmaceutical manufacturers could vertically integrate to control the supply chain and limit competition.

A business timeline system doesn't exist but should. E-commerce companies need to record all business events—product launches, website changes, sales promotions, email campaigns, paid advertising adjustments, and policy changes—on a single timeline. When analyzing performance months later, companies need context about what drove results. Comparing April 2025 to April 2024 requires knowing what happened each month: Which product launched? What conversion rate optimization changes were made? Which campaigns ran? Most e-commerce operators try to remember or rely on fragmented records across different tools.

The problem becomes more complex with multiple team members making changes, but assigning a single site owner to maintain the log solves accountability. Tools like Triple Whale or Heatmap could potentially add this functionality by integrating with email platforms, advertising networks, and analytics systems. Human input would still be required for non-integrated activities like podcast advertising or direct mail campaigns. This timeline could prevent repeating mistakes and clarify which changes actually moved the business.

Proxy contests represent another underserved business opportunity. When activist investors like Nelson Peltz pressure public companies to make strategic changes, the companies spend enormous sums fighting proxy battles. Disney and Procter and Gamble each spent over one hundred million dollars fighting Peltz-backed proxy contests. These battles require winning shareholder votes, which is essentially a marketing and reputation challenge. A specialized agency could emerge focusing exclusively on proxy contests, employing Google Ads, Facebook advertising, shareholder email campaigns, and media placement strategies to convince shareholders which way to vote. The minimum engagement value would likely be twenty-five million dollars given the stakes involved. Corporate executives defending their positions will spend whatever necessary from company coffers to retain their jobs. Firms already exist that advise shareholders like Glass Lewis and ISS, influencing how major institutional investors like Vanguard cast their votes, but a dedicated marketing agency for proxy battles could capture significant value.
