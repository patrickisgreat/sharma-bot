# Limited Supply Episode 3: Underrated Tactics and Strategies

Welcome back to Limited Supply. This episode extends the conversation from the first episode of the season, which focused on overrated tactics like loyalty programs and refer-a-friend programs. The feedback was tremendous, and today we're diving into the inverse: underrated tactics, strategies, and approaches that don't get enough attention but genuinely help make your day-to-day operations more efficient, improve your data, and generate more revenue.

## Leveraging AI for Creative

The first underrated tactic involves using AI tools beyond the obvious ChatGPT and DALL-E. Butter and Marpipe both leverage AI in their creative generation, allowing you to make creative at scale quickly. Butter is particularly interesting because it lets you take ad templates, insert your own renders or images, and use AI to remove backgrounds and turn images into 3D renders for ads.

TV ads.ai is another compelling platform. It ingests all your digital creative used on ads, banners, and websites, along with briefs and data on what's working for new customer prospecting. It then generates TV creatives, letting you test numerous TV ads instead of the typical one or two that most brands create.

Claude is worth exploring for copywriting, especially when trained specifically on your brand—it's stronger than ChatGPT for this purpose. For beauty brands specifically, flockshop.ai helps ensure products show up on different skin colors, making it easy for consumers to see how products would look on them rather than just seeing generic models.

## Setting Up Real-Time Dashboards

Eight or nine years ago, real-time reporting meant a team sending out a daily email with Google Sheets or Excel data pulled into Tableau. Today, the landscape is different. For smaller brands, general reporting apps can create real-time analytics dashboards. As you scale and add more channels and distribution points, business intelligence tools become essential.

Using a tool like Tido allows you to build a customized dashboard called Charmalytics that pulls everything from Shopify to Meta to Google to organic traffic, email, and SMS into one place. The beauty of BI tools is that they're built around your specific business. You can create custom metrics and math equations for your goals—contribution margin by email, profit per recipient, or whatever metrics matter most to you—rather than relying on generic reporting features.

## Affiliate Programs with Publishing Editors

This is one of the most underrated tactics. Most brands think about influencers and email lists as affiliates, but the best affiliates are editors at publications. Publishers give you access to their entire website and content, often pushed through their social channels and newsletters.

To leverage this effectively, you need to be set up on Skimlinks, which integrates directly with publishers' content management systems. When an editor writes content mentioning your product, Skimlinks prompts them to add an affiliate link—and they almost always will because it's revenue for them.

Beyond that, build direct relationships with affiliate managers at publications where your customers shop. Before every sale, notify them of timing, why it's great, and give them their own links and codes. You can even give them early access to sales before public announcement, making them feel like they have the upper hand and incentivizing harder pushes.

Direct relationships also allow for better program structures. You can create tiered commission structures where, for example, the first hundred sales earn one commission level, the next tier earns a higher rate, incentivizing continued effort. You might offer 35% commission when competitors offer 10%—this is often still cheaper than your Facebook spend. The key is making sure attribution windows are tight so you're not overpaying.

## Remove Honey and Capital One Shopping

Honey, the browser extension promising savings, is largely a scam. Most brands encounter Honey through their affiliate programs on platforms like Impact or ShareASale. Honey gets users to install a browser extension and then, at the very end of checkout, refreshes the page and inserts their affiliate link, taking credit for sales they didn't drive.

This means you pay them commission on sales driven through other channels—whether from paid ads, other affiliates, or creators. Honey removes other affiliates' credit and claims it for themselves. They promise access to a 20-million-person audience (a number from 2020) while their actual traffic contribution is often negligible.

The proof emerges when you assign them specific UTM codes. Looking at where actual traffic comes from reveals that Honey isn't driving visits; they're just taking last-click credit. When one brand analyzed their Honey relationship, they'd paid $2,500 in commissions without any actual traffic attribution. The browser extension simply activates at the last second before checkout.

Capital One Shopping and similar browser extension affiliates operate the same way. Double-check the UTMs and look at actual traffic sources before continuing relationships with these programs.

## Strong Brand Marketing and Messaging

Brand marketing deserves more attention because it directly impacts performance marketing. Brands that performed best last year weren't just good at paid acquisition—they had strong brands with clear tone of voice, messaging, and positioning.

Many brands think they need a full design rebrand when their real need is messaging and positioning refinement. A useful exercise involves answering 21 questions about your brand perception, available at nick.co/21questions. These questions cover everything from what your brand would post on Instagram stories to what it would retweet on Twitter. This helps nail down tone of voice and how you want to be perceived.

The exercise humanizes your brand by making it feel like a real person. Many brands focus heavily on product development and performance marketing but lack that human element, which can make an enormous difference in how customers connect with you.

## Investing in Organic Social Presence

Most brands gain followers as a byproduct of running ads, but you should treat every new follower like someone walking into your physical store. For younger audiences especially, your Instagram or TikTok profile is your storefront—sometimes more important than your website.

Instagram profiles should include highlights covering all main topics: reposts from customers (social proof), press mentions, FAQs, how products are made, and what makes them different. Every promotion should be announced across platforms, even if just as a story post.

Broadcast channels are an easy win many brands overlook. They let followers opt in to hear every update from your brand. Use Instagram to DM creators and build relationships—creators love hearing from brands that genuinely engage with them. Find your most engaged followers, give them early access to new products and sales, and they become long-term evangelists.

Close friends is surprisingly underutilized. Instead of just following your brand in general, creating exclusive content for close friends makes people feel special and more invested. This approach returns value many times over.

## Building Group Chats with Peers

Find your equivalents at different companies and build a group chat. Nine years ago, when one marketer reached out to directors of performance marketing at Third Love, Madison Reed, and Stitch Fix for monthly lunches, just the information shared netted at least six figures in revenue.

Moving this to an iMessage group chat creates real-time sharing. Everyone shares what they're seeing, learning, and testing daily. Group chats provide faster access to information than industry publications, newsletters, or podcasts—information often comes at least a day earlier. You get real-time insights on what peers are doing differently and potential breakthroughs applicable to your own work.

## Taking Meetings with New Vendors

Even if you're not immediately interested in a vendor's solution, meetings with new vendors reveal what's changing in your space. These companies are usually challengers coming in because they have some edge or capability current players lack.

For example, a meeting with TV ads.ai revealed how generative AI and ingesting your entire creative library could transform into TV creative—providing insights into how similar technology might evolve across Facebook ad creative and other channels. You don't need to commit to anything; you can simply ask for a demo and indicate you might be interested down the road. These meetings often reveal emerging trends and capabilities worth knowing about.

## Cleaning and Cutting Your Lists

There's a vanity metric around email and SMS list size—the feeling that bigger lists mean more revenue. In reality, platforms bill you per email and phone number, and you're exporting these unwieldy lists into ad platforms, sending bad signals to Facebook, Google, and YouTube.

A company like wastenot.tech identifies people you should remove from lists or stop including in ad targeting, making your overall targeting and ad spend more efficient. Over a year, cleaning an inactive list saves thousands of dollars. If you have 100,000 emails and 15,000 are inactive but you're sending two campaigns weekly to everyone, you're throwing away money on people who will never buy.

## Whitelisting

Whitelisting, used since 2014, involves running ads from accounts other than your brand name handle. If you sell a Dell monitor but run the ad through MKBHD's Instagram handle, that's whitelisting. This can be done through meme accounts, publishers like The Hustle or Business Insider, or creators and influencers. Verified whitelisted accounts typically reduce CPCs by 50%. Unverified accounts still provide significant drops. You can also run whitelisted ads from your own Instagram account as a founder or marketer—there's no reason not to at least test this approach.

On TikTok, the equivalent is Spark ads. Seed content to many creators, identify which ones perform above baseline, and run those exact posts as ads. Since they keep the original engagement, comments, and likes, they feel native to the platform—the goal of all advertising.

## Building Out a New Customer Offer

Many brands have beautiful websites and good ads but miss a crucial element: a compelling new customer offer. This involves merchandising what you're selling and why.

Start by identifying which product drives the highest sell-through for new customers. Export Shopify reports and ask ChatGPT to help identify the winner. Build an offer around this with an enticing discount, enough quantity for a household of four to try, and complementary products to raise perceived value.

For example, if selling an orgain protein powder normally priced at $39, bundle it with protein bars (easier to ship than liquids), add a free blender bottle, and throw in a month of a fitness app to increase perceived value. Price the entire bundle at $59 when the perceived value is over $100—creating a 40% discount impression while keeping the actual AOV lower.

Once you've tested a few offers and found the winner, scale new customer acquisition behind that specific offer. This works across industries: apparel, beauty, food and beverage, and more. The new customer offer approach also allows you to build focused landing pages and ads, find creators aligned with the offer, and create a better overall customer experience.
