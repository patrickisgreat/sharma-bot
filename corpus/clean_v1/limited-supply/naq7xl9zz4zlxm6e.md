# Limited Supply Podcast Transcript

The first area worth testing in Q1 is internal creators. Rather than hiring random creators through agencies, the better approach is to build a network of exclusive internal creators who work directly for your brand. These are contractors and freelancers who dedicate their time entirely to creating content for your company.

Internal creators provide significant advantages. The speed of production is remarkable—if you have an idea, an internal creator can produce finished content within one to two hours. They don't require separate editing teams since they handle editing themselves using standard tools. They understand which hooks are trending on platforms like TikTok, Reels, and Shorts, and they know how to apply those insights to your brand. Most importantly, they understand what's on-brand versus off-brand and have internalized your brand guidelines.

Many brands dismiss this role as too expensive, spending hundreds of thousands monthly on Facebook ads while refusing to invest five to twelve thousand dollars on an internal creator. This is shortsighted. A dedicated internal creator can produce five to ten videos per day with two to three going straight into ads. They also oversee and manage external creative partners, whether agencies, UGC from customers, or freelancers found on social platforms.

The whitelisting benefit cannot be overstated. When you whitelist an account belonging to your creator, your CPC decreases, your CPM drops, and your CTR increases. You can get an internal creator verified on their account for under fifteen dollars monthly, and running ads from verified accounts performs significantly better than unverified ones.

If you can't invest in a full-time internal creator, start by whitelisting your own account or creating a new account to test content. The time from ideation to a live ad can be less than thirty minutes. Compare this to working with an agency, where the minimum timeline is two to three weeks just to get the first concept approved and launched. If you had an internal creator, that same project for busy moms who need to stay hydrated could be completed in fifteen to thirty minutes.

YouTube represents another strong testing opportunity, particularly because most brands rely heavily on just two channels: Meta and Google. While these channels can carry brands to ten to twenty million in revenue, eventually diversification becomes necessary for reaching bigger revenue goals.

YouTube works well for driving awareness, especially when coupled with other performance channels like Meta and Google. In holdout tests focused on ad reads and pre-rolls, YouTube consistently performs. When combined with Google and Meta, YouTube typically shows measurable results within six to ten weeks, providing strong evidence to increase investment.

The most effective YouTube strategy involves creator sponsorships and partnerships. These are ads read within creators' actual videos, where the creator reads from start to finish. The most successful execution keeps the main content flowing with the sponsor message integrated naturally throughout, which keeps viewers engaged and prevents skipping.

The advantage of YouTube sponsorships over programmatic YouTube ads is longevity. A programmatic ad is served once to an individual viewer. A creator sponsorship, however, stays in the video forever. Creators like Graham Stephan have videos about personal finance that accumulate views indefinitely, meaning sponsorships like those from Morning Brew continue generating new subscribers and sales years later.

YouTube is also expanding into creator commerce and affiliate programs. Brands can integrate with creators and have products linked permanently in video descriptions. For example, Ridge Wallet works extensively with tech reviewer Marques Brownlee, who has Ridge products linked in videos and has even created custom designs. He earns affiliate commissions on all sales, typically three to nine percent of customers' entire carts, not just individual products.

Never give creators a script to read. You can provide guardrails and bullet points, but verbatim scripts sound inauthentic and fail. When you step into someone else's world, they need to speak natively to their channel. When forced to read a script, audiences immediately recognize it as a bad ad. Give creators freedom within parameters, and the results will perform far better.

TikTok represents multiple opportunities across TikTok Live, TikTok Shop, and TikTok affiliate programs. While TikTok Live performance has declined from its peak—brands that once made sixty to hundred thousand per live now make significantly less—the arbitrage opportunity still exists. TikTok Shop and affiliate programs remain strong and are actively growing.

TikTok's strategy of keeping fees low, currently around ten percent and expected to increase, has made the platform attractive for brands across categories. Supplement brands, apparel brands, and sellers of lower-priced items all perform well. The key is understanding how to work with creators and produce content that feels relevant, engaging, and interesting enough to capture and hold viewer attention.

The optimal approach involves product seeding combined with creator partnerships. Seed your product to many creators and let forty-seven different creators share forty-seven different perspectives on why they use the product. This might include a mom sharing how it helps her stay hydrated, an athlete discussing performance benefits, someone weightlifting mentioning strength gains, someone explaining daily hydration improvements, and another person highlighting sleep benefits. Whatever content performs above baseline gets amplified with paid ads while giving creators a percentage of the upside. This becomes your best advertising because it comes organically from real users.

The biggest benefit of TikTok that rarely gets discussed is middle-of-funnel performance. While top-of-funnel and bottom-of-funnel metrics are easier to measure, middle-of-funnel is where most brands fail. This is when potential customers search for the brand on Google, TikTok, or ChatGPT to verify legitimacy—to ensure the brand is trustworthy and won't steal their credit card information, and to confirm the product actually delivers on its claims.

You cannot manufacture middle-of-funnel proof. People go to TikTok, Reddit, and YouTube to find authentic, unsponsored validation. This is where seeding products and building presence in TikTok Shop becomes invaluable. Brands like Cadence Capsules and Eight Sleep executed this perfectly. Eight Sleep made a major YouTube investment years ago when they realized people needed proof that smart mattresses actually work. That investment continues paying dividends daily as potential customers verify the product's legitimacy before engaging with paid ads or sponsorships.

Advertorials represent a powerful but underutilized channel. Advertorials combine advertising with editorial content—essentially long-form articles distributed through publisher sites or your own branded publisher sites.

The basic structure involves buying a domain with a generic name like dailyhealth.co or dailywellness.co, setting up WordPress or Webflow with a clean design, and publishing customer stories and educational content. The user experience should be frictionless: easy fonts, quick load times, no pop-ups, and no obtrusive ads. This contrasts sharply with major publishers where ads consume most of the screen space.

The content itself should tell real customer stories or provide education related to your product. These stories might highlight founder narratives or customer experiences, and they should be genuinely truthful rather than fabricated. Formats can include listicles, personal experience pieces comparing products, or "I saw this trending on Instagram so I tried it" narratives.

The strategy involves driving traffic from a whitelisted Instagram and Facebook page to your advertorial site. You place a Facebook pixel on the advertorial site, allowing retargeting of users who don't immediately click through to your product. Typically, five to fifteen percent of visitors click through directly to your landing page. The remaining eighty-five to ninety-five percent can be retargeted either with partner offers from the whitelisted page or special offers from your main brand page.

Advertorials work best for products that are somewhat difficult to explain and require education, higher AOVs, or higher price points relative to the category. If you meet two of these three criteria or all three, advertorials should perform well. The beauty is the speed of implementation—you can have a complete advertorial website with testing ads live within six hours. You can write an article at night and have it live the next morning.

Whitelisting amplifies advertorial performance significantly. Create whitelisted pages for different content themes—DIY fitness tutorials, meal prep content, lifestyle advice—and test them. This approach is so effective that Facebook's disruptors team, a specialized unit for high-growth brands, reported that advertorials doubled their share of media spend because they're easy to produce, work effectively, and audiences prefer them to traditional ads because they're entertaining content rather than explicit advertising.
