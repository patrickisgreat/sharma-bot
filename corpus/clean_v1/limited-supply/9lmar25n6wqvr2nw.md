# 40 Underrated Tactics to Drive Better Conversion Rate

When thinking about improving conversion rates going into the holidays, it's helpful to use the metaphor of preparing for the best possible at bat. You need to tie your shoes, make sure your gloves fit, ensure the Velcro is on, and make sure the helmet is snug. These forty tactics represent all the preparation you can do before trying to hit that home run.

## Social Proof and Customer Voice

Start by leveraging more social proof from real world humans rather than modeled and paid actors. While user-generated content creators are everywhere now, try to figure out how to use your actual customers instead, ideally through reviews that include video content.

When you do include customer quotes on your site, make sure they call out benefits rather than just features. Don't put a quote like "I love the teal color and that the bottles are machine washable." Instead, use something like "Upgrading to the 64 ounce tumbler changed my life. I drink more water today than I ever have before. I feel much healthier and I have more energy to sleep better due to being properly hydrated from this bottle." These quotes should speak to lifestyle change and aspirational benefits that resonate with potential customers.

When customers leave reviews, tell visitors what they bought next to their review. Include details like "Nick bought three teal tumblers from Simple Modern" along with a verified purchaser badge. This adds credibility and specificity that helps other shoppers make purchasing decisions.

## Copy and Messaging

Don't be chunky with your copy. Avoid overly wordy and jargony language. Instead, focus on being punchy and short, highlighting benefits rather than value propositions. The best writers constantly rework sentences to delete words. The more you can say with fewer words, the better your copywriting becomes.

Design and write copy for mobile first. This means when you're ideating and deciding what content goes in, what photos you'll use, and what button sizing you'll employ, everything should be designed for mobile initially and then fitted back to desktop. You might find that a headline that looks great on desktop takes up three or four lines on mobile, so rewriting for mobile first ensures better clarity on all devices.

For any product, reduce the amount of text whenever possible. This applies to your headlines, descriptions, and overall page copy.

## Pricing and Offers

Make your pricing, discounts, and offers way more noticeable. Use different colors, bold fonts, highlights, or stickers to make pricing pop. Consider adding the discount information to your calls-to-action and using colored CTAs, which significantly outperform black and white ones. Make sure your deals are very obvious throughout the page—in the shop section, in the CTA, and with badges on product images.

Put your very best offer at the top of the page. This could be in a banner bar, the first bundle on a collections page, the hero module on your homepage, or a pop-up that collects emails. Make sure it's the loudest thing on the page and call out those savings in your advertising as well.

If you sell a consumable product, include the price per use. For example, if you're selling a 30-day supply for $28, say "less than a dollar a day." If you're selling 36 bottles for $36, highlight that it's a dollar a bottle. This helps customers justify the purchase and gives them a talking point when explaining their purchase to others.

## Visual Design and Hierarchy

Elevate your site design. If your site doesn't look beautiful, you're not building trust, and there's no reason to drive traffic to a site with poor design. Elevated design equals trust, which equals better conversion rates.

A picture is worth a thousand words, so use more of them. Include illustrations, icons, graphs, and charts to explain things visually. This works especially well for supplement and beverage companies. Custom illustrations and icons tend to get more engagement, and icons with movement draw even more attention.

Establish a proper hierarchy in your text sizes using H1, H2, and H3 tags to create visual hierarchy. This makes it very easy for someone's eyes to follow the page. The Apple website is an excellent example of how to do this well.

Whenever possible, use real life images rather than just studio photography or stock imagery. Lifestyle imagery, especially for products related to luggage, apparel, jewelry, and style, helps significantly with conversion.

## Product Information and Details

Tell your customers what's inside the box. Better yet, show them with photos or even a UGC video. This is often a huge thing that deters people from making a purchase because they're not sure what actually comes in the box.

On product pages, include videos showing your product in action. Videos can increase conversion rate substantially. Include founder videos if possible, and use thumbnails that clearly indicate what the video demonstrates. If someone wants to understand how to style a bag, the thumbnail should clearly show that's what the video demonstrates.

Always have a comparison chart. You've never seen a comparison chart on a site that didn't make you feel more educated about the purchase. While some brands compare themselves directly to competitors, it's often better to create comparison columns that show general categories like matcha versus coffee without naming specific competitors, or premium versus standard without naming brands. These charts help keep people on your page rather than sending them to search for comparisons elsewhere.

## Trust and Assurance

Always add shipping information and make it very clear. Answer the questions: if I buy today, does this ship today? And when does a product likely arrive at my door? Name your shipping rules descriptively, like "Easy Breezy Shipping (ships within 24 hours, arrives within 3-5 days)" rather than just generic names. Add this information near your add-to-cart button and consider using slowly blinking green lights with text like "Orders shipping now" to build trust.

Never hide the return information. Understanding what happens if you don't like the product is one of the biggest things that will drive a purchase. Make clear whether there's a 30-day guarantee, if it's easy to make a return, and what the process is. Don't make it hard to find on your site.

If there's a 15, 30, or 60 day money back guarantee, highlight it prominently. Make it very obvious what the return policy is and how many days the guarantee covers. This works especially well for products targeting men, who want assurance they can get out of the purchase if unsatisfied.

Add social proof directly to your checkout screen. As someone checks out, include messages like "500 happy customers also bought this" or "30,000 orders shipped" or "100,000 customers around the globe." Something that reassures them they're not making a mistake and that others have successfully received products before them.

## Email and Pop-ups

Make your email popup form much simpler. Don't ask for their name, just their email address. The best approach is to use engagement-driven popups where they answer a question about what they're looking for, then provide their email. After getting the email, you can ask for additional information like phone number or birthday.

Make your popup subscribe form a value-added experience. Offer a discount, enter them into a raffle, or make sure you're running a promotion in the popup. If you're running a Black Friday promotion, highlight that in the email popup so customers think they need to provide their email to get the offer.

Be aware of email collection software that rejects emails already in your database, which can frustrate returning customers. Try to mitigate this issue before the holidays.

## Checkout and Purchase Experience

Simplify your checkout experience. A customer should never have to make more than two to three clicks to go from what they want to buy to checkout. Use a slide-out cart rather than taking customers to a separate cart page. This is especially important for low AOV products where you want to encourage multiple item purchases.

Site speed is critical to this process. Fast loading improves both AOV and UPT because customers are more inclined to keep adding items when the experience is quick and easy.

Turn on BNPL (buy now pay later) options. You can use Shop Pay Installments powered by Affirm, or other services like Klarna, Sezzle, or Quadpay. Affirm is preferred because there are no late fees, meaning customers won't associate late fees with your brand. BNPL usage increases during the holidays as more people look for efficient ways to buy.

Test upsells and cross-sells both pre-purchase and post-purchase. Test them as customers add products to the cart through pop-ups offering complementary or adjacent items, in the cart itself, during checkout using checkout blocks, and post-purchase. Testing the same product as a post-purchase offer often performs well as customers are likely to buy it again.

Don't say "buy now" on your CTA—use "add to cart" instead. For some reason, this just converts better and feels less intimidating.

## User Experience and Accessibility

Design for mobile first. Most site traffic comes from mobile devices, so everything should be optimized for mobile and then fitted back to desktop. The best designers create one experience for mobile and another for desktop rather than just fitting one to the other.

Make sure everything on your site is fully ADA compliant. Run ADA checks with online software or services to ensure your site is accessible to everyone. Avoid getting sued with frivolous claims by being proactive about compliance.

Optimize your text and visuals for the most visited screen size, which for most brands is an iPhone. Check your analytics to confirm what your most visited screen size is.

Study your heat maps to see where users are bouncing and falling off. Use heatmap.com to view heat maps, scroll depth maps, and user session recordings. The platform ties buttons to revenue so you can see which CTAs are driving the highest amount of revenue. This helps you understand when to move sections or reorganize your page layout.

Study your dead clicks and rage clicks with heatmaps. A dead click is when someone clicks on something that doesn't lead anywhere, like clicking a product image on a collections page when the actual link is on the title below. Rage clicks are multiple clicks in a short period when someone gets upset nothing happens. These are easy opportunities to convert missed revenue by relinking elements.

## Brand and Strategy

Describe your brand as a person. Try to figure out what time this person wakes up, what they do when they wake up, whether they go to nice dinners, if they're fashionable, and so on. Give them a name, like "Carly is bougie and loves fancy dinners and traveling." Use this persona as a filter for your ideas—does it feel like this person would do that? This helps with checks and balances on your content.

It might sound cool in the brand book, but if it makes no sense to the customer, cut it. Get gut checks from people outside your internal team like agencies, partners, or contractors. If you've been part of building your brand book, you probably have romantic ties to what's in there and understand everything about it, but the average person might not care or understand it.

Organize your site the way you would organize a physical storefront. What items do you want customers to see first? What associated photography and messaging do you want them to see? Try to figure out what has high sell-through for new customers and prioritize that, assuming returning customers already know what they want.

## Testing and Simplification

Make sure a third grader or your grandma can understand your product, offer, how to check out, and buy from your site. If your site's too complicated, go back to the drawing board and simplify it. You can also try the "drunk test"—if someone has had a couple drinks, can they figure out your site? If not, simplify.

Make sure your site answers six key questions: What is the product? What benefits will they get? If they buy it now, how soon will it arrive? How does it compare to competitors? What happens if they don't like it? And what is the return policy? If these are answered, you should convert people.

Page load speed should be under one second. Customers bounce from slow-loading pages, especially those coming from in-app browsers on Facebook, Instagram, and other platforms. These browsers are slower, so you need to be extremely fast.

Never forget a FAQ section. It's one of the best ways to answer questions and ensure confidence right before purchase. Make sure you're truly answering frequently asked questions rather than just filling in cute or on-brand answers.

Test variations. This is the single most important thing you can do. Make duplicates of your page and split test copy, imagery, and offers. The only way to boost conversion rate is to improve it by one percent at a time. Use the same traffic source to run clean experiments with one variable. Over six to twelve months of testing, these small improvements compound into doubling your conversion rate.

## Compliance and Legal

Don't highlight limited time offers unless they're truly limited time. If it's really your always-on customer acquisition offer, highlighting it as limited time is now against FTC rules. You'll get in trouble for this.

Tell customers how many have purchased a product, but be truthful about the numbers. You can say "5,000 plus happy customers" or "285 people love this product," but don't make up numbers. This is against FTC rules and you will get caught and face consequences.
