# Limited Supply Season 11: Interview with Liam, Founder of Instant

## The Early Days: Navigate Australia

I started my first business when I was sixteen. My mom, dad, sister, and I went traveling around the world for four years full time. I couldn't go and get a job at McDonald's or something like that for people my age, so I started this online travel magazine called Navigate Australia. It grew to fifty thousand readers in twelve months and we partnered with about one hundred ninety councils and tourism brands.

The biggest thing was we just created very relatable content, both on social and in the magazine. People started to naturally or organically share it amongst each other, and it just went from there. We were paying people to travel, having them write for the magazine and create content. It was just naturally evolving. That was my first business and kind of set the foundation for what came next.

## The Pivot to E-Commerce

COVID hit and everyone stopped traveling. Advertisers pulled out of the magazine and it naturally died. I was sixteen, seventeen at that point, and I went to university to study business and marketing. One of my marketing assignments involved looking into the recent growth of e-commerce towards the middle of COVID. We needed to set up a Shopify store, dropship products from China, advertise them on Facebook ads, and see if we could use our newfound marketing skills to generate revenue.

I chose to do pet beds and pet products because everyone was buying new dogs and cats in COVID. It went from an assignment to doing ten to twenty thousand dollars in our best month. This was pre-Shop Pay, and I found that the majority of people landing on the website weren't converting at the point where they needed to put in their personal information.

That's when we built our first product called Quick Checkout, which would remember a shopper every single time they came back, giving them an accelerated checkout experience. That's how Instant came about.

## From Checkout to Retention

We started with a product called Quick Checkout, then renamed it to Instant Checkout. We built technology that allowed us to remember a shopper every single time they came back to your website or any other website in the network. As we evolved, every single shopper we remembered became more and more valuable by the day as we started remembering shoppers wherever they went to purchase across the internet.

We were increasing conversion and allowing shoppers to accelerate through checkout. But then the competitive environment shifted. We wanted to get into the Shopify ecosystem and couldn't because of Shop Pay being a very prominent player. Shopify makes the majority of their revenue from payments and they're super protective of it.

We realized we had built this technology that allows us to remember a shopper wherever they're going to purchase across the internet. Instead of using it in payments, we wanted to use it on the retention and marketing side. That's where the pivot happened for us as a company. We built audiences and grew to five hundred brands in less than twelve months using the product.

## How Audiences Works

Audiences matches the shoppers that visit your website with a deliverable email address, so you're able to retarget and remarket them after they abandon. We remember your opted-in shoppers for longer, opening a whole new revenue channel for brands.

Most browsers, pixels, and platforms like Klaviyo and Facebook have what we call short-term memory loss. They remember a shopper for very short periods of time—in some cases just one day. Instant remembers your opted-in shoppers forever. If they come back to your website in seven days, a year, two years, or during your Christmas sales period, we remember who they are, allowing you to bring them back to convert after they've left your website.

## Why Instant Can Do What Others Can't

For us, it's kind of our core strength. Klaviyo, for example, is very focused on campaigns and email editing, allowing a brand to set up their emails. They're not very focused on remembering a shopper—it's just not their core business. For us, it's all we focus on.

We use server-side cookies and multiple data points to track a shopper, but we also make sure every single shopper is opted in and has a very high intent of purchasing. We look for signals and behavior of a shopper—say like they've come back to your website multiple times, they've added an item to their cart, maybe they've scrolled around for a long period of time. These are really high intent signals, and we use that information to trigger an email at the perfect time.

## Privacy and Compliance

People definitely raise concerns about identifying visitors without getting their explicit email address, but we really fight against the messaging that's so common in this space. So many players have done the wrong thing by buying huge lists of consumer data and plugging it into a merchant's Klaviyo, which causes spam rates and unsubscribes to skyrocket.

We prioritize the fact that we only remember opted-in shoppers. If a shopper comes back to your website, most of our brands' email platforms help them remember ten to fifteen percent of their returning shoppers. Whereas Instant helps you remember sixty to seventy percent of your returning opted-in shoppers when they come back. But the key thing is that every single shopper is opted in somewhere—whether they've purchased before, filled out the email newsletter, or subscribed. They have to have been opted in before, and we allow them to unsubscribe at any time.

As a venture-backed business, we follow so many rules and there are audits internally and externally. We've been put under the microscope by massive brands' legal teams, which keeps us on our toes. Australia is a very complex country from a consumer privacy perspective, as is the U.S. Being legal here in Australia ticks a lot of the same boxes as we expand into the U.S. The biggest thing is making sure that every single shopper that enters our network and that we share with a brand is opted in.

## Key Features and Use Cases

We work with incredible brands like Selfie Leslie and Toys R Us, who use Instant to retarget shoppers leaving their website. We've almost doubled their email revenue channel since onboarding, increasing their revenue by over a million dollars in some cases after about eight months.

## Pop-Up Design and Timing

Internally, we've seen patterns around best practices for pop-ups. I think it comes down to the offer. Shoppers are pretty likely to enter in their email address no matter what the design looks like, as long as the offer is compelling. Free shipping is huge. The best brands do twenty percent off your first order. But displaying the pop-up at the right time is critical.

Most brands display a pop-up immediately as you enter the website, and almost every shopper just clicks the X button. Then when they're ready to purchase, they can't find it. What we do is build up a point system where certain interactions build up points for that shopper when they're on your website. Once they reach a point threshold based on their behavior, we're likely to show a pop-up. Those points signal towards a shopper being very likely to purchase—they've added an item to their cart, they've come back to the website multiple times, or maybe they've gotten all the way to entering their credit card information but exited out of their cart. We've seen that the timing of the pop-up can double the conversion rate of the opt-in.
