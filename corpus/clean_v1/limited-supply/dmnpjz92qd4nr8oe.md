# Limited Supply Podcast Transcript

When it comes to active subscribers, there are multiple ways brands can create a positive experience that makes customers feel thoughtful care is being extended toward them. One approach uses quick actions in emails. For example, a brand might send an email saying, "Hey, Nick, we see that you're on an active subscription and we're doing a Black Friday sale. Apply that to your next renewal order." The customer can press a claim button, and the discount gets automatically applied to their next subscription order without requiring manual intervention.

Alternatively, brands can apply bulk actions where the discount is automatically extended to every active subscriber in the database. The communication might read, "Hey, Nick, we just wanted to let you know we're having a Black Friday sale. We've already applied it to your subscription. Thanks so much. We appreciate you." This approach removes friction and demonstrates that the brand is thinking about its most loyal customers.

WellBell, a physician-formulated daily supplement for hair, skin, and nail health, operates under the philosophy that pricing and discount strategy must be carefully balanced against customer lifetime value. The company is not one that heavily discounts throughout the year, with Black Friday being their largest sale annually. The approach recognizes that the industry must pay to play during this critical period, as consumers expect substantial discounts, but WellBell remains protective of margins while seeking quality customers who understand the product requires a minimum three-month commitment to see results.

In December of the previous year, WellBell shifted their strategy away from heavy discounting in favor of a referral incentive program. They offered a "give fifteen, get fifteen" promotion where customers could stack discounts from multiple referrals. A unique feature is that customers who refer six friends receive a free jar. This program required custom development with Friendbuy because existing platforms couldn't support it at the level of sophistication WellBell needed. The automatic application of stacked discounts removes friction from the customer experience.

There's significant untapped potential in refer-a-friend programs that few brands have explored. Modern technology could enable more seamless referrals, such as group chat integrations where a customer could invite a friend through a messaging platform and have a WellBell phone number automatically apply the referral code. Similarly, QR codes embedded in digital wallets would eliminate the friction of copying long alphanumeric codes. Some companies like Novel are already building loyalty programs directly into Apple Wallet, removing friction points that plague traditional referral mechanisms.

Pricing strategy requires balancing multiple objectives. One key mistake brands make is offering site-wide discounts greater than subscriber discounts, which undermines subscription value and causes customers to cancel and re-subscribe to access better pricing. WellBell avoids this trap. During peak seasons, alternative incentives like gift-with-purchase items have proven highly effective. Jolie, for instance, leveraged beautiful tote bags as GWP items during Black Friday with significant success. The strategy preserves margin while creating perceived value for customers.

One of the most powerful but underutilized tactics is implementing discount offers directly into the cancellation flow. When a customer indicates they want to cancel, displaying a special offer at that critical moment can prevent churn. For example, if a customer's reason for cancellation is that they saw a Black Friday sale elsewhere, the flow can respond with, "Oh no, here's our offer. You don't need to cancel. We're sorry you missed our email. We actually want to give it to you." This approach has achieved twenty percent save rates for some brands.

The cancellation flow itself requires strategic differentiation. WellBell discovered that monthly and quarterly subscribers have different considerations and should journey through distinct cancellation experiences. By building specific flows for each SKU and subscription length, the brand can meet customers where they actually are. When a customer is three months into a monthly subscription without seeing results, WellBell offers a different approach: getting on a Zoom call to discuss why results aren't appearing and whether the product is right for them. This human touch contrasts sharply with standard cancellation processes.

Customer service philosophy profoundly impacts retention. WellBell has built their business around human interaction as a core value. While beautiful automation tools exist, the brand chose not to implement a chatbot because of significant customer backlash when they tested one. Customers explicitly asked for Emily, the community service manager, by name. This human-first approach resonates more powerfully than AI efficiency. WellBell would rather have Emily step into customer service herself during peak times than compromise on the human element, even though it requires more resources.

The rise of text-based customer service presents both opportunity and challenge. While platforms like Recharge offer SMS support capabilities, the challenge is getting customers to understand they can text a brand. Unlike email addresses where support@ is universally understood, or phone calls where customers know what to do, SMS adoption requires education. There's no default understanding that someone can text a brand to manage their subscription. Creative redirects like wellbell.com/text-emily could build awareness, but the fundamental behavioral pattern hasn't been established.

Subscription management through SMS remains underdeveloped across the industry. The ability to delay a subscription by texting, change frequency, or adjust next shipment dates would solve real customer problems. Many customers hold excess inventory because they consume product slower than their subscription frequency. Rather than canceling entirely, they'd prefer to temporarily pause or delay their next shipment. SMS could make this frictionless, but adoption requires overcoming the psychological barrier that texting a brand isn't a normal behavior.

Social media support and community management during peak seasons requires integrated systems. WellBell uses Gorgeous, a platform that aggregates all social media handles and feeds all inquiries into one central dashboard. Emily manages responses across Instagram DMs, email, comments, and any other communication channel, ensuring consistent voice and quick response. Notably, one of the company founders, Stacey, steps into social media support over weekends, signing messages with a purple heart so customers can identify it's her versus Emily's red heart signature. This personalization creates a distinctive brand experience.

Instagram presence requires specific optimization going into peak seasons. The bio should clearly communicate what the brand sells, who it's for, and include significant social proof. Leading with review counts or other metrics like bottles shipped or number of happy customers establishes credibility. Highlights should be organized strategically, with current promotions appearing in the leftmost highlight position for immediate visibility. The URLs in the bio must be updated to match current campaigns, and ideally, the last line of the bio mentions the current promotion as well. This prevents confusion when customers click through from ads only to see no mention of the sale on the profile.

Landing pages are critical infrastructure during high-traffic periods. Every major promotion should have its own dedicated landing page. Even simple click-through interstitial pages explaining the offer, how to claim it, and delivery timing significantly improve conversion. This is particularly important because roughly sixty percent of Black Friday traffic consists of new customers, people on the fence from prior marketing, or those referred by friends. These customers need education about what the brand sells, why it matters, who they are, and whether they can trust delivery.

A sophisticated approach to Black Friday spans multiple days with distinct offers and landing pages. Early access for VIP customers might arrive Wednesday evening with positioning emphasizing exclusivity. Standard early access could launch Thursday. Friday features the main Black Friday sale. Small Business Saturday, often overlooked, works well for cross-selling other product categories. Sunday could feature another category. Cyber Week benefits from a tiered promotional structure where early orders receive the deepest discount, creating urgency.

The question of when to start Black Friday promotions has shifted based on consumer behavior data. Google's analysis revealed that the average consumer has approximately twenty-four hundred dollars in annual wallet share available for holiday shopping, gifts, and discretionary purchases. This spending begins earlier than many brands realize. If a brand wants to capture five percent of that wallet, starting promotions in October makes sense rather than waiting until November. However, September feels premature for most categories except gift-giving alternatives.

The nine-for-ninety-nine strategy at Hint demonstrates how limited assortment can support higher discounts while protecting margin. By pre-kitting nine specific flavors—three best-sellers, three middle-performers, and three inventory-clearing SKUs—the brand could offer approximately fifty percent off during Black Friday. This approach served multiple purposes: it moved excess inventory, attracted deal-seekers, and created clear product bundles that simplified decision-making. Customers unwilling to accept the limited selection were encouraged to wait for additional sales launched Monday.

Gift-with-purchase and cashback alternatives deserve consideration as replacements for traditional discounts. Cashback is particularly advantageous because it reduces net revenue rather than gross revenue. If a brand offers ten dollars in cashback but the cost of goods sold represents thirty percent, the actual cost is only three dollars. Additionally, digital gift-with-purchase options—like partnerships with Othership meditation apps or similar services—create richer customer experiences while potentially reducing margin impact compared to flat percentage discounts.

Operational preparation for peak season requires specific timelines and planning. WellBell began preparing in July for a season running from September through early January. Starting planning early prevents expedited shipping fees, reduces warehouse stress, and maintains professional relationships with partners. The brand prefers to have inventory and materials in the warehouse a month early rather than stress about late delivery and pay premium fees. This proactive approach prevents the dynamic where a brand is constantly asking for favors and apologizing to partners.

Inventory forecasting and warehouse capacity demand cross-checking historical data. WellBell examines last year's SLA times and delays, compares them to the year prior, and uses that intelligence to have conversations with warehouse partners about expected performance. The brand expanded to two additional warehouses this year in California and Georgia in addition to their original New Jersey location, cutting transit times in half from five days to two and a half days. An SLA commitment of same-day shipment for orders placed before noon doesn't ship the next business day but must go out same-day, requiring precise coordination.

Route, a package protection service, handles delivery issues, loss, theft, and damage claims. Critically, Route's customer support handles these issues directly with customers ninety percent of the time without the brand needing to get involved. This removes a massive source of customer service inquiries during peak season. The brand can run reports to understand whether damage is increasing, whether communication is needed with the 3PL, and to identify patterns. Post-purchase tracking through both Route and text message integration into the post-purchase journey keeps customers informed.

ShipBob, used by WellBell, doesn't observe blackout dates, meaning inventory can always be delivered, though it experiences delays in checking inventory into the warehouse. The brand manages pipeline closely by splitting deliveries with manufacturers rather than taking one large shipment, allowing them to take advantage of high-volume pricing while maintaining inventory control. This approach ensures the brand never faces the stress of counting jars and holding their breath about stockouts.

Custom packaging requires planning. WellBell is exploring custom tape strips because 3PLs can't print custom tape directly. Pre-cut tape strips arrive in boxes and are placed at picking stations for workers to apply. Managing inventory of these custom strips requires tracking usage across workers. Holiday inserts and branding elements are also being planned for launch before the season. The brand acknowledges that custom holiday packaging has diminishing perceived value for consumers—most unbox a package in seconds without photograph or social media sharing—but premium products benefit from thoughtful presentation. Most consumers don't post unboxing videos of holiday boxes to social media the way some brands hope they will.

Sustainability and eco-friendly messaging, while heavily marketed by major brands, rarely rank as primary purchase drivers. Research across multiple brand tests shows that when people rank value propositions, sustainability consistently ranks last, below pricing and convenience. This doesn't mean eco-friendly packaging is unimportant, but brands should be realistic about its impact on purchasing decisions and not overinvest in eco-friendly infrastructure at the expense of core customer needs like reliable delivery. Some brands like Buffy have built sustainability into their DNA effectively using recycled cardboard and vegan paint, but these remain exceptions rather than industry norms.

TikTok Shop represents the strictest platform requirements for shipping and product approval. Uploading products to TikTok Shop can take days to weeks for approval. Once approved, maintaining reputation requires shipping all packages within twenty-four hours of order placement with tracking number submission. Failure results in TikTok canceling entire pallets. This requirement demands clear communication with 3PLs, tags identifying TikTok orders for prioritization, and proper staffing. Some fulfillment centers have shifted to six-day operation weeks and are moving toward seven-day operation specifically because of TikTok Shop requirements.

Facebook and Instagram use page quality scores informed by customer surveys. Facebook sends surveys to customers asking whether they received their package on time and whether they had a positive experience. Yes and no answers weight the overall page quality score, which displays as a letter grade. Poor scores result in dramatically increased CPMs. If customers order Friday and shipments don't go out until Wednesday the following week, the brand faces page quality score deterioration and rising ad costs. Brands can request removal of complaints if they can prove they communicated delays proactively.

Google Shopping also enforces shipping and customer service standards. Brands must accept returns for at least thirty days from purchase or delivery, or Google removes them from Shopping listings. These platform requirements increasingly shape operational priorities. Most brands don't track or compile these requirements, yet they directly impact profitability. A comprehensive guide documenting platform-specific SLA requirements, return policies, and approval processes would be valuable resource for the industry.

The intersection of marketing and operations becomes especially critical during peak season. Marketing drives traffic and customer acquisition, but operations must ensure delivery, customer service quality, and fulfillment speed. These systems must be synchronized. A marketing campaign that succeeds in driving twenty percent more orders is only successful if operations can fulfill them within the promised timeline and maintain quality standards. Brands that ignore this relationship face page quality score drops, platform penalties, and customer dissatisfaction that compounds future acquisition costs through damaged reputation and reduced ad platform performance.
