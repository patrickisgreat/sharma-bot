# The VMS Boom: What the Fastest-Growing Supplement Brands Are Actually Doing

If you've been paying attention to the e-commerce landscape, you've probably noticed an explosion of activity in the vitamin and supplement space. Brands like IMA, Groons, Lemmy, Grey Matter, and the Absorption Company are absolutely ripping right now. They're raising capital, getting acquired, and scaling faster than almost any other DTC category.

Over the last several months, I've been deep in the trenches working on a couple of brands in this space, and I've basically documented everything these winning companies are doing. I'm talking about seven core things that separate the brands that are crushing it from everyone else.

## The Seven Core Principles

Let me start with the foundation. If you're doing at least five of these seven things, you're in a good spot. If you're doing all seven, you're in a great spot.

**One: Treat Your Product Page Like a Classroom, Not a Checkout**

The old DTC playbook was all about urgency and speed. Add a timer, throw in some aggressive copy, get people to convert as fast as possible. That's dying. The winning brands right now are focused on education and long-term customer value. They understand that if you educate someone properly on why they should buy your product, they're not just more likely to convert, they're more likely to stay as a customer and refer others.

This represents a fundamental shift back to thinking about lifetime value instead of just customer acquisition cost. For years, the industry was obsessed with CAC. But the companies that are winning today—the ones getting funded, getting acquired, still scaling profitably without raising insane amounts of capital—they're thinking about LTV. They're treating the product page as an educational asset where you're building a case for why someone should commit to this product for the long term.

**Two: The 90-Day Subscription as the New Default**

This is brilliant. Instead of trying to get someone on a monthly subscription, the best brands are pushing quarterly subscriptions. Why? The economics are just better. When you ship quarterly instead of monthly, your shipping costs drop dramatically. You're also avoiding the triple hit of credit card processing fees across multiple transactions. Plus, you get better unit economics on acquisition because you have a higher initial order value.

Brands I've studied are also experimenting with six-month and annual subscriptions. The math is simple: higher initial AOV means more margin, which means you have more room to acquire customers profitably.

**Three: The ALO Framework—Ad, Lander, Offer**

This is where the real sophistication comes in. With Meta's Andromeda update, the game has completely changed. It's no longer about taking one piece of creative and testing ten variations of it. Meta now wants you to create 25 completely different ads.

Think about it differently. You need an ad about fiber, an ad about menopause, an ad about sleep, an ad about athletic performance. Each of those ads needs to match to a landing page or product page that speaks the same language and continues that exact message journey. And then you need an offer that ties it all together.

This is where congruency becomes critical. If someone clicks on an ad about how your supplement helps with digestion and bowel regularity, they should land on a page that continues that exact conversation. You don't send them to a collections page or a generic homepage. You send them to a page that doubles down on the digestion conversation and then introduces your product as the solution.

**Four: Quiz Funnels**

Quiz funnels were huge from 2017 to 2019, kind of fell off during the pandemic, but they're roaring back. Meta just acquired Octane, the leading quiz platform, which tells you everything you need to know about where this is headed.

Quiz funnels crush conversion rates when you have multiple product options. Think about it from a brand like Ritual or Hims or Bioma—they have tons of SKUs and options. A quiz helps people figure out which product is actually right for them. That's incredibly powerful for conversion.

But here's the real kicker: quizzes are also amazing for health and wellness brands because they work within the compliance restrictions around data and tracking. When you run a seven-question quiz, you can identify intent patterns from the answers, and those become legitimate conversion signals that feed back to Meta. That's why health companies especially lean on quiz funnels.

**Five: Industry-Level Endorsements, Not Just UGC**

Anyone can get a UGC creator to talk about their product. That's not what I'm talking about here. I'm talking about real credibility. Doctors. Scientists. People who have built careers and reputations in your space.

Look at IMA—they have doctors from Mayo Clinic on their advisory board. Haya has Dr. Joel. Momentus works with Andrew Huberman and David Goggins. These aren't just paid endorsers. These are legitimately credible experts.

Some brands also layer on third-party credibility badges like Front Row MD, which aggregates reviews from actual clinicians. Good Anya, one of my favorite electrolyte brands, has an entire section on their PDP dedicated to Front Row MD reviews. Venus Williams' protein company went even further and integrated a clinician AI chatbot that answers questions about ingredients and potential interactions with medications.

**Six: The Offer**

This is probably 40 to 50 percent of your funnel. Your first-order welcome kit or offer needs to create an experience that stands out. Some brands offer free Tumblers, some offer free travel tins, some offer three months of a meditation app subscription like Calm.

What works best is a combination: product plus a couple of gifts plus some level of discount. We're going to dive deeper into the specific offers and percentages, but the point is that your offer needs to be tested and validated, and once you find something that works, you lock it in as your control and test different landing pages and ad angles against it.

**Seven: Smart Subscription Management and Churn Reduction**

The default on your PDP should be subscription, but here's the key—you need to make it crystal clear what you get in a subscription versus what you get in a one-time purchase. What are people missing out on if they don't subscribe?

Seed does this better than almost anyone. They have skip-before-cancel flows that give people options. You don't want someone to cancel when they could just skip an order or try a different flavor. And then exit surveys feed your internal R&D with critical information about why people are leaving.

One thing that's criminally overlooked is the actual interface and design of your subscription management portal. If you just use the out-of-the-box tool from your platform without customization, people are going to churn because it's hard to use or it doesn't match your brand. Technology companies and newspapers absolutely nail this. The Wall Street Journal, for example, has incredible cancel flows where they'll offer you a discounted month if you stay.

## The Top 10 Funnel Types That Work

Beyond those seven core principles, there are specific funnel types that are proven to work at scale.

**Quiz-Gated Intent Funnels**

You see this all the time in telemedicine. Hims, Roman, Ro—they all use variations of this. The quiz helps you understand intent and it works around compliance issues with data tracking. If you're in health or wellness, this is probably the most powerful funnel type you can deploy.

**Founder or Origin UGC**

Ritual does this incredibly well. The founder gets involved or there's UGC from the very beginning of the company. People connect with that. It feels authentic. These funnels convert really well.

**Doctor-Led Endorsements**

The entire funnel is built around one credible doctor or expert. The ads feature them, the landing page shows their recommended bundle or their favorite flavors, it's all about their endorsement and their expertise.

**Competitor Comparisons or Comparicles**

"Groons vs AG1" or "Huel vs MyProtein." These work because they're meeting people where they already have interest. Maybe they've heard of AG1 but not your greens powder. This shows why yours might be better. Meta loves these because they're high-intent content.

**Problem, Agitation, Solution UGC**

Brands like Love Wellness and Lemmy do this really well. You lead with someone saying "I have this problem." The viewer thinks "I have that problem too." Then you agitate it a little—what makes this problem suck—and then you introduce the solution. It's pure sales fundamentals translated to video.

**Ingredient-Led Mechanism Visuals**

Brands like Thorne, Element, and Armra do this. You show the ingredients and visually illustrate how they work. This works especially well if you've bought branded ingredients that have clinical studies behind them.

**Advertorial Native Strategy**

These are editorial pieces that feel like stories people can relate to. "Seven Reasons Why Home Chefs Can't Stop Cooking With Carraway" type headlines. The genius is that you're not leading with the product. You're leading with the problem or the benefit. Someone reading about brain fog when they scroll isn't necessarily thinking "I need your supplement." But now you've got permission to introduce it as the solution.

Advertorials make up 15 to 40 percent of ad spend in the supplement space, and for good reason. They help you find new audiences who have implicit intent even if they don't know they're in the market for your category.

**Stat-Led Editorials**

Brands with real clinical data and trials use this. The entire editorial is built around the clinical evidence. This is incredibly powerful if you have the science to back it up.

**Creator-Led Video With Promo Code**

Usually lower funnel, but incredibly effective for building social proof. If someone has been seeing your brand in ads for a few weeks and then they see an influencer they trust talking about it too, that psychological effect is real. Running multiple creator videos with different promo codes creates this sense that the brand is being talked about everywhere.

**Meme Strategy**

Some brands actually run memes as ads. It's not the most effective for everything, but if it works for your brand voice, the engagement and conversion rates can surprise you.

## Ad-to-Landing Page Congruency

The biggest thing tying all of this together is making sure your ad matches your landing page perfectly. This has been massively accelerated by generative AI in the last few weeks. Whether you're using Claude, Codex, or whatever else, you can now create massive volumes of matched ad and landing page combinations.

If you run an ad about menopause and how your powder helps with that specific issue, the landing page needs to continue that exact conversation. You don't send people to your homepage. You don't send people to your general collections page. You send them to a page that doubles down on the menopause angle and then introduces your product as the solution.

Meta doesn't like incongruent funnels. More importantly, your customers don't like them. You'll just end up paying more for lower conversion rates. The winning strategy is matching ads to matched landing pages.

## Building the Perfect Product Page

Now let's talk about what your product carousel should actually look like. Most brands have at least five images, but eight to ten is better. People typically engage with the first five to six, but if you've hooked them, they'll scroll through all ten.

The formula that's working best right now goes like this:

**Slot One: Hero Product Image with Text Overlay**

This is your main product shot, probably with some text calling out the main benefit or value proposition, maybe some icons showing key features.

**Slot Two: Lifestyle Shot**

Someone using the product. Could be a celebrity pouring it. Could be a UGC creator mixing electrolytes into water. Could be someone actually holding the product in a real context.

**Slot Three: Ingredient or Benefit Infographic**

This is where you show the eight or nine ingredients you use, or the main benefits someone will get. Think of it as a mini listicle format that shows what makes your product special.

**Slot Four: How It Works Visual**

Do you scoop and shake? Do you open and take? Or is it about timeline—by week two you'll notice this, by day 30 you'll notice this. This is critical because most supplements don't create an immediate sensation. People need to know what timeline to expect.

**Slot Five and Beyond: Proof and Credentialing**

Quotes from doctors, badges from NSF or other third-party certifiers, clinical trial results, Mayo Clinic logos because you have their doctors on your board. Whatever third-party credibility you can show—not just claims you're making about yourself.

**Additional Slots (if you have them):**

Welcome kit flat lay showing everything someone gets with their first order. Competitor comparison chart. Founder shot with a quote about why they started the company. Screenshot collages of real customer reviews from Okendo. UGC videos. Another infographic. Whatever else helps your case.

Think of your carousel as a landing page. Different sections, all working together to educate and convince someone that your product is the right choice.

## Above-the-Fold Elements That Matter

Here's what needs to be visible before anyone scrolls:

Review count and star rating next to your product title. It's shocking how many brands don't have this, but it's literally one of the first things people look for.

A supplement panel or nutritional panel showing every ingredient and dosage. And if you have clinical dosages, call them out. If you have 200mg of an ingredient and the clinical dose is 180mg, label it "Clinical Dosage for X."

Expert endorsement above the fold. A doctor quote, a third-party badge, something that immediately signals credibility.

Buy box default set to subscription, but with crystal clear information about what you get in the subscription versus one-time purchase. What's the price difference? What are they missing if they don't subscribe?

Price per day framing. "Less than $2 a day" is way more compelling than "$55 a month." You want to make it easy for people to justify the purchase.

Strikethrough pricing showing the one-time price next to the subscription price.

Trust bar below your buy box. Third-party tested? Money-back guarantee? No artificial colors? Whatever trust signals you have, show them.

Quantity and variance selector if applicable.

Risk reversal line. "30-day money-back guarantee" or whatever guarantee you offer.

## Below-the-Fold Content

Below the fold, the best brands are including these sections:

**Who Is This For?**

"If you're a woman in her thirties dealing with afternoon brain fog, this is for you." Explain it simply. Don't make it complicated.

**The Problem**

"77% of Americans take supplements, but 90% remain deficient." State the problem clearly and concisely.

**How Does It Solve It?**

Plain English explanation of how your product is better than what's already on the market.

**Ingredient Breakdown With Dosage and Citation**

Show your work. Every ingredient with its dosage and scientific backing.

**Clinical Trial Modules**

If you have them, show them. If you don't, use clinical trials from the branded ingredients you bought.

**Comparison Chart**

Easy-to-understand visual comparing your product to competitors or alternatives.

**Timeline to Results**

This is critical and so many brands skip it. Supplements don't work like prescriptions. It might take two weeks to feel effects. Say that. Set expectations. It reduces returns and complaints.

**Another Layer of Social Proof**

Customer reviews, UGC videos, whatever you've got.

**FAQs With Real Objections**

Not the standard "What's your shipping policy?" FAQs. Real questions your customers are asking. The actual objections they have.

**Final CTA With Guarantee**

End strong with a clear call to action and whatever guarantee you're offering.

## Mobile-First Design Is Non-Negotiable

Here's the reality: 70 to 85 percent of your traffic is mobile. If you design for desktop and then cram it down to mobile, you've already lost. Design for mobile first.

Make perfect use of the real estate. Use horizontal swipe with dot indicators. Have a sticky add-to-cart button. Make sure your cart knows how to upsell. Collapse the right things and expand the important things.

When someone plays a UGC video on your PDP, don't open the native Apple player full screen. Let it play nicely within your page.

Optimize your image file names, use good alt text, and make sure your photos load fast. File size matters on mobile.

Don't put bullshit on your page. Don't add fluff just because you can. Every element should serve a purpose. Every image, every section of copy—it should all be driving toward understanding and converting.

## The Bigger Picture

What's happening right now in the VMS space is a return to fundamentals. For years, the DTC world was obsessed with acquisition at any cost. You raised capital, you spent it on ads, you tried to grow as fast as possible. The metrics that mattered were CAC and top-line growth.

That's changing. The brands that are winning now are profitable, sustainable, and focused on long-term customer value. They're treating their customers like they want to keep them for life, not just extract one sale. That means education. That means congruent messaging from ad to landing page to product page. That means real credibility and real science backing up their claims.

It's a better way to do business, and the market is rewarding it. Brands that do this are scaling faster, getting funded, getting acquired, and building real businesses. Brands that are still stuck in the old playbook are getting left behind.

The specifics matter—the carousel order, the trust signals, the guarantee language—but the principle is bigger. You're building a case for why someone should care about your product and commit to it. Not tricking them into a purchase. Actually earning their trust and their business.
