# Limited Supply - Season 2, Episode 8: Black Friday Post-Mortem

Growing your business is hard, but scaling a Shopify store profitably requires the right tools and insights. This episode dives deep into Black Friday performance data and best practices from one of the biggest shopping days of the year.

## Black Friday Performance and Trends

Across 2,500 U.S. Shopify stores, Black Friday sales were up 28% compared to the previous year, with orders up 18%. The average order value reached $106. These same-store sales comparisons provide apples-to-apples data showing genuine performance metrics rather than inflated numbers from new store additions.

Advertising spend increased across most platforms, with Google up 43.5%, TikTok up 25%, Pinterest up 29%, and Facebook up 23.3%. Snapchat was the only platform to see decreased spending, down 30%. The CPM increases were notable as well, with Facebook CPMs up 22% during Black Friday week compared to the prior week, and TikTok up 8%.

One surprising trend involved non-Shopify stores sending emails claiming their sites had gone down due to technical issues and offering discount codes as apologies. These plain text emails generated significantly higher click-through rates than traditional styled marketing emails, sometimes three times higher. One brand that sent such an email on Saturday had more sales from that single message than they'd achieved on Black Friday itself, increasing revenue by 10%.

## Email and SMS Strategy Observations

Brands blasted customers with an unprecedented volume of emails and text messages throughout the Black Friday period. The recommendation for subscribers is to downgrade Klaviyo and other email management systems back to normal usage levels after the promotional period ends.

A critical insight emerged around email timing and time zones. Many brands sent emails at 7 a.m. Eastern time to all subscribers regardless of location, which buried West Coast recipients and wasted impression share. Brands that performed better sent emails based on recipient time zones or chose times when customers were actively checking messages, such as after 4 or 5 p.m. Eastern when people got off work. For newsletter sends, Sunday evenings around 7:15 p.m. work well because people aren't being blasted with promotional emails.

Retargeting email openers who haven't converted proved highly effective. The strategy involves sending an initial email, then waiting 36 hours before retargeting via ads on Thursday. This gives customers time to make a purchase decision while keeping ad costs low. Brands repeated this process with each new email campaign throughout the Black Friday week.

## Discount Strategy and Positioning

There's no shame in discounting on Black Friday. Even premium brands like Apple offered discounts during the holiday period, sometimes extending promotions beyond Friday through Monday. The brands criticizing others for discounting are missing the point—customers expect discounts, and offering them is participating in the cultural event of Black Friday shopping, not diminishing brand value.

Gift with purchase strategies proved particularly effective, especially when structured with tiered spending thresholds. Setting the discount threshold just above typical average order value incentivizes customers to add more items to their carts rather than simply reducing the price of what they were already going to buy. For example, offering a free item at $300+ spending encourages basket building when typical AOV is $50-100.

Some brands notably avoided traditional percentage discounts. Jolie, the showerhead company, didn't discount at all and saw revenue up 117%, though cost per acquisition dropped 47%, showing that smart media spending can compensate for no promotional offer. Viore offered free gifts rather than percentage discounts, maintaining better price integrity while still providing perceived value.

## Content and Creative Strategy

Brands that performed exceptionally well treated Black Friday as a content game rather than a one-off promotional event. Instead of running the same few ads repeatedly with changed redirect URLs, successful brands constantly created new ad variations and launched them continuously throughout the promotional period.

On the website itself, successful brands kept things fresh and exciting. Rather than keeping the same homepage and simply changing backend promotions, they re-merchandised frequently, launched new product bundles, renamed existing bundles with new packaging, and added dynamic elements like popups and header bars emphasizing the Black Friday sale. This approach mimicked an advent calendar effect where customers had something new to discover each day.

Product categorization and bundling deserved attention. Some categories performed exceptionally well—cookware brands saw 48-111% revenue increases on relatively modest spend increases of similar percentages. Food and beverage showed particularly strong performance, with one condiment brand seeing 92% spend increase and 100% revenue increase. Apparel proved more challenging, with margins compressed by competitor discounting and commoditization of products.

## Quarterly Performance Leading Into Black Friday

From October 1st through November 20th, even before Black Friday, brands experienced strong growth. GMV was up 20%, orders up 10%, and average order value up 8%. Advertising spend across all platforms was up 24%, with TikTok spending surging 81% compared to just 23% increases on Facebook. This TikTok surge during Black Friday preparation will be worth monitoring in 2023 to see if brands continue allocating budget toward the platform or revert to more traditional channels.

## Customer Insights and Conversion Optimization

The most successful Black Friday campaigns clearly demonstrated how a purchase made sense and wasn't a frivolous decision. Brands showed products in context, displayed how items looked in use compared to ads that drove clicks, and made the value proposition crystal clear. This was particularly important for gift purchases, where buyers worry about looking foolish or giving something that appears silly.

High AOV products performed surprisingly well. One apparel brand targeting customers willing to spend over $400-500 achieved a 6.5% conversion rate on Black Friday, resulting in $5.5 million in revenue for the single day, with most orders coming in the evening. This suggests customers were intentionally shopping for big-ticket items during the promotional period.

## The Real Business Model Lesson

An instructive example came from K-Jewelers, which discovered its actual business model was warranty insurance, not jewelry. While selling $4,000 diamond rings with modest margins, the $200 warranty insurance was pure profit and almost never claimed because diamonds don't degrade or get lost. The company collected substantial cash upfront while spreading warranty profit across multiple years for accounting purposes. This lesson applies to DTC businesses: understanding where actual profit comes from versus where revenue comes from is critical to strategic decisions.
