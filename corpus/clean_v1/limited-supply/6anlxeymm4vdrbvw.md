# Limited Supply Season 11 - Bottom of Funnel and Commerce Experience

Welcome back to Limited Supply. Today's episode is a continuation of last week's episode, which was derived from a newsletter about how to focus on scaling a brand. Today we're diving into the bottom of funnel portion, whereas last week we covered the top of funnel.

## Proper Digital Ad Execution

When auditing ad accounts, the most common issues I see are bad account structure, outdated customer and exclusion lists, lazy naming conventions, no testing methodology for creative or audiences, and no innovation testing of alpha or beta features from Meta, Google, or TikTok. The worst offender is not leveraging post IDs across campaigns. Whether you're spending seventy thousand, seven hundred thousand, or seven million a month, you need a tightly run ad account. Poor account structure leads to poor data ingestion on the algorithm side, resulting in messy tracking and bad analytics. Getting the ad account structure right is foundational.

## Rapid Creative Testing

This goes hand in hand with good media buying. Usually less than twenty-five percent of any creative you put into an account ever sees scale. Most ad accounts have only one to three or four ads running the majority of spend. Focus on putting out more ad creative and testing what can work. It's a numbers game to figure out which ones will find scale.

Once you find something that works, like a static ad, keep the image and test twenty-five different text variations. Do the reverse too—keep the text and try different imagery, graphics, or layouts. Video offers even more possibilities. If you find a video that works, keep the hook and test different content behind it, or vice versa with different products.

Testing audiences, messaging, and offers—AMO tests—is critical. Constantly test different audience types: broad, lookalikes from first-party data, lookalikes from second-party data. On the messaging side, finding new angles opens and widens your TAM. For example, you'd message differently to a woman in Manhattan than a gym bro in Wyoming for a creatine supplement.

Offer testing is equally important. Don't just drive people to your homepage or product page. Figure out offers and merchandising that give new customers the best experience. Maybe it's a bundle with all top-selling flavors or enough product for the whole household to sample.

## Individual Product Page Funnels

Catalog ads are consuming increasingly more spend, and all major platforms aggressively push them because they're easy to serve creatively. Whether pulling from existing retailer content or using tools like Marpipe to create catalogs at scale, all platforms love catalog ads.

Product pages need to function as funnels themselves, like landing pages. Every good landing page answers five questions: Why are you selling this? Why should I care? How fast can I get it? How will it improve my life? How does this compare to competitors? If your product pages answer these five questions, your catalog ads will be significantly more effective. Optimizing creative with tools like Marpipe will also drive higher click-through rates.

## Landing Pages

It's 2024, and brands still aren't using landing pages to speak to different consumers in different ways. Your homepage speaks one way about your product, but you're running ads to different interest groups, demographics, and purchase motivations. Landing pages let you actually speak to these different groups.

You can use landing pages as listicles—five reasons why your creatine gummy belongs in every gym bag. Comparicles compare two brands in article form. Bundle pages combine three creatine gummy flavors. Social proof heavy pages work great for higher AOV products like mattresses or electronics. Shoppable blog pages allow articles with built-in shopping modules.

All these landing page types should be built directly inside Shopify in a modular fashion, following Shopify 2.0 standards. This allows interns or non-technical people to duplicate pages, iterate, add or delete sections, move them around, copy sections from product pages to landing pages, and make edits from your phone if coded back to meta fields properly.

## New Customer Offer

Hint's thirty-six bottles for thirty-six dollars, Everyday Dose's fifty percent off first order, and Harry's five dollar shave trial work because they get fence-sitters over the edge. Hint normally costs two to three dollars a bottle retail, but one dollar a bottle for thirty-six bottles across top-selling varieties is irresistible. Everyday Dose's fifty percent off is compelling. Harry's five dollars is less than a coffee.

For consumable product brands especially, figuring out a honey hole acquisition offer is the best way to scale customer acquisition. The offer becomes your control when running new channel experiments. If this offer works best on Meta and you want to launch TV, you run the same offer as your control while the channel and traffic behavior becomes the experiment.

## Website and Checkout Optimization

Most brands use Shopify templates or themes, with rare exceptions using fully custom websites. Everything should be tested and iterated: homepage modules, above-the-fold PDP sections, slide-out carts, navigation, collections page organization, and checkout extensions. Use tools like IntelliGems for A-B testing and analysis. Incorporate data points from heat maps, session recordings, scroll depth maps, post-purchase surveys, and ad copy performance into your testing.

I remember a hair care brand where one tiny button test in the cart generated an extra fifty grand in one week, translating to two hundred thousand dollars monthly incremental revenue. If you have lots of traffic, do small tests. With less traffic, do larger tests. You might be one test away from increasing conversion rate by twenty-five, thirty, or fifty percent.

## Upsells and Post-Purchase Offers

Use these to drive higher AOV and more items per cart. If someone buys a toothbrush, upsell toothpaste and push subscription. Once they check out, Shopify holds their payment token, allowing you to show a pop-up offering another toothpaste at thirty percent off. Take rates typically run twenty-five to thirty percent here.

This is revenue you're not paying for acquisition-wise since you've already secured the conversion. It's additional revenue on top, so it dramatically boosts gross and bottom line numbers.

## Subscription Optimization

This isn't just about having a subscription program—it's about clearly demonstrating benefits and reasons to subscribe on your website. You need to answer why someone should add another recurring credit card charge when consumers dislike subscriptions.

Sometimes the answer is straightforward, like the convenience of having Hint Water shipped rather than carrying six cases from the store. Don't overthink it. Figure out what's relevant to your customers and how they shop, then communicate that value clearly.

## Email and SMS Opt-in

If you're collecting below seven percent of site traffic opting in to email, focus on increasing opt-ins. Also use email fields to collect additional data. Brands like Hay Day Skin Care ask quick qualifying questions upon email entry, then customize welcome flows for both email and SMS, making content more relevant and boosting open rates, click rates, and ultimately order rates.

## Review Collection and Syndication

Collecting reviews is one of the best things you can do. Some brands have thirty-two thousand plus positive reviews on single products. Copy like "over X five-star reviews" or "over X happy customers" is tremendously effective. It competes with lines like "sold out, now back in stock for limited time."

Collecting customer feedback through reviews is crucial. Set forecasts for reviews just like you would for sales and revenue. Track whether you're pacing ahead or behind, and investigate why. This accountability ensures you're maximizing review collection.

Syndicate reviews everywhere possible—to TikTok, marketplaces, Target.com, and other platforms. The more you can spread social proof, the better. Great awareness and marketing traffic means nothing if your commerce experience isn't ready to convert easily. The "drunk guy test" applies: if a drunk person can figure out how to buy from you, anyone can.

## Measurement

There are multiple types of measurement to understand. In-platform reporting uses data Meta collects from websites, apps, and MetaShops to create models shown in dashboards. This data isn't one hundred percent accurate—it's model data.

MTA—multi-touch attribution—includes tools like Triple Whale and Northbeam. MMM—marketing mix modeling—uses tools like Prescient. Incrementality testing uses work platforms like Measured, Paramark, and House Labs. Different businesses benefit from different approaches.

The key is understanding how many points of sale and distribution you have (your website, Amazon, Ulta, Target, Costco, Sephora, etc.), and where you're advertising (Meta, Google, YouTube, TikTok, Snap, Pinterest, TV, billboards, podcasts, influencers). Based on this, decide how tight your tracking needs to be.

If you have multiple retailers and advertising channels, run MMM and incrementality testing. If you're only running media to your website, MTA and Facebook's internal incrementality tests work. All these measurement types play different roles and should be treated as data points. None are one hundred percent accurate, even incrementality, which requires continuous testing as markets adapt.

We recently tested add-to-cart campaigns on Amazon versus dot-com. On dot-com it underperformed, but on Amazon it performed well. That's specifically how Facebook runs those campaigns today. In six months, rerun the test to see if performance remains effective and if you need to recalibrate the incremental ROAS for different lookback windows.

This covers everything from top of funnel through bottom of funnel, commerce experience, and measurement. The goal is leveraging all these elements together to maximize the impact of your customer acquisition efforts and conversion rates.
